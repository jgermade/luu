# The 16GB threshold, and the two sixteens this project owns

**Status:** WIP — a protocol and an argument, waiting on the runs in *The runs*
below. Nothing here has been measured on this project's hardware. The machines
are available the morning of 2026-09-17 and this file exists so that the
afternoon does not get spent deciding what to type.

**Provenance.** The prompt is an external article — [*The 16GB
Threshold*](https://augmentedmind.substack.com/p/the-16gb-threshold), Manolo
Remiddi, 2026-09 — read in full and quoted here, **not** a measurement of this
repository. Under `local-first`'s fourth obligation (*every measurement carries
its backend or it is not a measurement*) nothing in it may be cited as a number
this project holds. It is a claim by someone with the same card, which is why it
earns a record rather than a link.

Two halves, and they are in one file because the same number is load-bearing in
both: **this project owns two machines labelled 16 GB and they are not the same
sixteen.**

## What the article claims

One GGUF — [`ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-GGUF`](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-GGUF),
IQ3_S equivalent at 3.44 bits per weight — on an RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB:

| build | KV type | ctx | VRAM used / 16 GB | t/s |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| IQ3_S, 11.77 GB | q8_0 | 98 304 | 15 437 MiB | 30.35 |
| IQ3_S-mtp, 12.12 GB | q4_0 | 65 536 | 15 089 MiB | 42.5–44.9 |

The mechanism it credits is **weight packing, not KV**: GSQ quantizes each tensor
at its own bit depth, RCO allocates depths under a total size budget, so the
result is non-uniform where Q4_K_M is uniform. The quality claim — *"performs
like the unquantized version"* — is scoped by the author to this build and no
other, and that scope is repeated here because it is the whole load-bearing
sentence: *"Buy the packing, not the file size."*

The secondary claim, about the architecture rather than the packing, is the one
that matters more downstream: Qwen3.8 is hybrid (linear/SSM layers plus full
attention), so 32K → 96K costs ~2.5 GB and generation speed stays flat. Context
is nearly free in a way it is not on a dense transformer.

## Part one: the RTX, and a conclusion that was right

[`the-14b-context-ceiling`](2026-09-08.the-14b-context-ceiling.completed.md)
closes with:

> **Not Qwen3.8 anything.** … the 27B doesn't fit this card's weight budget
> regardless of KV-cache type.

That is correct and the reasoning behind it is correct: ~17 GB of Q4 weights
against 16 311 MiB of card, and **no KV-cache trick touches a weight budget**.
The record reached it after rejecting TurboQuant for the right reason, and
nothing in the article refutes it.

The article does not refute it — it **routes around it**. GSQ/RCO is the other
half of the sentence: it attacks the weights, which is the term the record
correctly identified as untouchable *by the thing it was holding*. 17 GB at
uniform Q4 becomes 11.77 GB at non-uniform 3.44 bpw, and 11.77 GB fits 16 311 MiB
with room for the KV sweep that record already published.

So the open item in that file is not wrong, it is **answerable for the first
time**, and the same box that closed the 14B ceiling can close it.

Against what is already measured there, which is what makes this cheap:

| | measured, 2026-09-08 | claimed by the article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| weights | 8 571 MiB (14B Q4_K_M) | 11.77 GB (27B IQ3_S) |
| comfortable | q8_0 @ 49 152 · q4_0 @ 65 536 | q8_0 @ 98 304 |
| edge | q4_0 @ 98 304, 1 154 MiB free | 874 MiB free |

The interesting row is the last one. The article's *comfortable* is this
project's *measured edge*, on the same card, and
[`the-floor-on-6gb`](2026-09-08.the-floor-on-6gb.completed.md) already showed
that band moving with the desktop compositor's own VRAM. Under 1 GB free is not
a default on a machine with a screen attached, whoever measured it.

## Part two: the BC-250's nine gigabytes are a setting

[`the-bc250-run`](2026-09-04.the-bc250-run.completed.md) corrected the inventory
from "14b comfortable" to "7B comfortable" on the strength of three numbers, and
that correction stands. What it got wrong is **the kind of fact those numbers
are.** It called the split *"a board-level carve-out"* and treated 9.4 GiB as a
property of the board. It is a property of the board **as configured**, and the
record's own arithmetic says so:

```
free -h                     →  9.4 GiB system RAM
Vulkan heaps                →  3.57 GiB  +  7.14 GiB DEVICE_LOCAL
llama-server --list-devices → 10 973 MiB

3 655 + 7 311 = 10 966 MiB ≈ 10 973
```

**The 10 973 MiB is not the VRAM.** It is the device-local carve-out plus the GTT
window into system memory, summed. And 7.14 + 9.4 ≈ 16.5: the whole board is
accounted for, only partitioned.

On a discrete card that partition is a cliff, because the far side is DDR behind
PCIe. **Here both sides are the same GDDR6 at ~224 GB/s** — a PS5-derived APU has
one pool. Moving the line is accounting, not bandwidth.

That reinterprets a number in the original record. The `-ngl 5` run at 11.8 t/s
against `-ngl 99` at 55.3 t/s was **not slow because of memory**; it was slow
because Zen 2 cores were doing the matmul. The lesson is not *stay inside the
carve-out*, it is *stay on the GPU* — and raising GTT does exactly that: same
compute, more addressable memory.

If the line moves far enough, the BC-250 goes from *the 27B cannot fit* to *the
27B is a measurement*:

```
Qwen3.8-27B GSQ/RCO IQ3_S = 11.77 GB of weights

as configured today   9 814 MiB free  →  does not fit, before one KV token
with GTT at 12–13 GiB                 →  fits, with little left for KV
```

**Estimated, not measured**, so that nobody quotes it back as a number: at
224 GB/s ÷ 11.77 GB, times the ~0.8 efficiency implied by the article's own RTX
figures (448 / 11.77 = 38 theoretical against 30.35 measured), the ceiling is
**~15 t/s**. Half the RTX, which is what half the bandwidth predicts. Usable and
slow.

## Part three: the rival thesis, which is the part that touches the design

The article is not an endorsement of this project. It argues the opposite of the
differentiator, in one sentence:

> Every compaction is a lossy event … The work quality on long tasks improves
> not because the model is smarter, but because it forgets less.

That is **buy window, do not compact**, against this project's *a smaller,
better-chosen context beats a larger, worse-chosen one*.

[`local-first`](2026-09-01.local-first.completed.md) already took this hit once,
from the remote 256K window, and answered it honestly:

> That is a claim, it can be false, and the project now has to demonstrate it
> instead of inheriting it from the hardware.

The article brings the same question to *local* hardware, where there is no
rhetorical escape — and, unexpectedly, hands over the instrument to settle it.
Until now the window was the machine's, so "8K versus 96K" meant changing the
box, and `machines.md`'s organising rule forbids reading anything off that. With
one server started at `-c 98304`, the window becomes **`luu`'s own flag**, and
the comparison is one flag apart for the first time.

[`the-7b-does-not-miss-it`](2026-09-14.the-7b-does-not-miss-it.completed.md) is
the shape of the answer, one size down.

## The runs

Ordered by what blocks what. The 11.77 GB pull is the long pole, so it starts
first and the BC-250 work happens while it downloads.

### 0 — start the download, then go to the other machine

Both files from `ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-GGUF`: the base
`…-IQ3_S.gguf` (11.77 GB) and `…-IQ3_S-mtp.gguf` (12.12 GB). **Pull
`tokenizer.json` for Qwen3.8 in the same trip** — it is not the Coder family's,
so [`the-rtx-holds-14b`](2026-09-08.the-rtx-holds-14b.completed.md)'s shared-tokenizer shortcut does not apply here, and a
budget counted with the wrong ruler is not a budget.

### 1 — BC-250: how much of the board can the GPU address?

No model needed. The whole run is a heap reading before and after.

```sh
# before
free -h; llama-server --list-devices

# kernel cmdline, MiB and 4 KiB pages; 12 GiB of GTT
amdgpu.gttsize=12288 ttm.pages_limit=3145728 ttm.page_pool_size=3145728

# after
free -h; llama-server --list-devices
```

**Read it as:** does the number `--list-devices` prints move, and by how much.
Reverted by removing the parameters and rebooting. Boot headless if the desktop
is up — `the-floor-on-6gb` measured the compositor moving this band.

The firmware carve-out is the *other* knob and is deliberately not attempted
first: some BC-250 boards expose it and some are locked, and it is not revertible
by a reboot. Establish what the free knob is worth before touching the one that
is not.

### 2 — BC-250: will `ggml-vulkan` use the heap it was given?

The trap this run exists for: RADV exposes both heaps, but the backend may insist
on `DEVICE_LOCAL` for tensors and fail rather than spill. **A bigger number from
`--list-devices` is not the same as a bigger model loading**, and confusing the
two would be this record's version of the mistake Part two is correcting.

```sh
llama-server -m Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-IQ3_S.gguf -ngl 99 -c 8192 \
  --cache-type-k q4_0 --cache-type-v q4_0 -fa on --jinja --port 8080
```

**Read it as:** loads / OOMs at load / loads and OOMs generating. Then a real
turn, never a load check alone — the standard this repo already holds itself to:

```sh
luu chat "what does the context manager do?" \
  --backend openai --openai-url http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1 \
  --model qwen3.8-27b --context-limit 8192 --reserve 512 \
  --tokenizer ~/models/qwen3.8-27b/tokenizer.json
```

Two failures are expected here and are findings rather than problems: **IQ3_S on
RADV** has had uneven support and may be slow or refused outright, and `-fa on`
with a quantized V-cache is CUDA-tested in this repo and Vulkan-untested. Either
one closes roadmap item 1 with a *no*, which is an answer.

If the 11.77 GB build will not go, the 8.4 GB variant is the fallback — the
article places it above 8 GB and below 12, which is exactly this board's band.
**Its quality claim does not carry**; the article scopes *near lossless* to
IQ3_S alone. A fit there is a fit, not a result.

### 3 — RTX: does it fit, and does it run?

```sh
# range profile, the article's own flags
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 llama-server \
  -m Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-IQ3_S.gguf -ngl 99 -c 98304 \
  --cache-type-k q8_0 --cache-type-v q8_0 -fa on --jinja \
  --temp 1.0 --top-p 0.95 --top-k 20 --min-p 0.00 --port 8100

nvidia-smi --query-gpu=memory.used,memory.total --format=csv,noheader
```

**Read it as:** the VRAM figure against the article's 15 437 MiB, and against
this repo's own edge row (`q4_0 @ 98 304`, 15 147 MiB, 14B). Then a real turn
that makes the model **propose and execute a `run_command`**, the same validation
`the-rtx-holds-14b` and the ceiling sweep used — a context that loads can still
OOM while compute buffers grow.

Observe in passing, do not chase: whether a 27B emits a ` ```tool ` block without
the spelled-out prompt the 7B needed. That belongs to
[`a-grammar-for-tool-calls`](2026-09-06.a-grammar-for-tool-calls.WIP.md).

Then the MTP profile, for the speed/range pair:

```sh
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 llama-server \
  -m Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-IQ3_S-mtp.gguf -ngl 99 -c 65536 \
  --cache-type-k q4_0 --cache-type-v q4_0 -fa on --jinja \
  --spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-n-max 2 --port 8100
```

**Not `--model-draft`** — that loads a second full model and dies on one card.
The draft block is inside the `-mtp` file.

### 4 — RTX: does the 27B hold the corpus?

The 38 questions, one per file, scored against `scripts/tasks/map-order-probe.key`:

```sh
cargo test -p luu --test select_probe -- --nocapture
```

and the selection run that
[`does-the-model-read-it`](2026-09-06.does-the-model-read-it.completed.md) scored
33/38 on with `qwen2.5-coder:7b`.

**Read it as:** against the key, never as a delta against the 14B. Both are
scored on the same key on disk, which is legitimate; calling it *one flag apart*
would not be, because the model, the family, the packing and the tokenizer all
moved. `machines.md`'s organising rule applies to a model swap as much as a box
swap.

### 5 — the one this record is really for: 8K against 96K, one flag apart

One server, started once at `-c 98304`, and **`luu`'s own `--context-limit` as
the only thing that varies.** The window belongs to the server — the caveat
`the-bc250-run` printed and verified — so this is the first comparison in this
repository where the window is a flag rather than a machine.

```sh
for limit in 8192 98304; do
  luu chat --script scripts/tasks/grounded.txt \
    --backend openai --openai-url http://127.0.0.1:8100/v1 \
    --model qwen3.8-27b --context-limit $limit --reserve 512 \
    --tokenizer ~/models/qwen3.8-27b/tokenizer.json \
    --record ~/records/2026-09-17.grounded-$limit.jsonl
done
```

**Read it as three outcomes, and say which one before looking:**

- **96K answers questions 8K gets wrong** → the rival thesis holds on this corpus,
  and the differentiator owes an argument it does not currently have. This is the
  outcome that costs the most and is the reason to run it.
- **They tie** → the project's claim survives its first local test and wins on
  tokens, which the recordings will quantify.
- **8K wins** → surprising, and would want a second corpus before it is believed.

At 98 304 nothing evicts and nothing folds on a 20-prompt script, so this is
honestly *context management on* against *context management irrelevant*, not
against a rival implementation. Worth stating plainly rather than discovering in
the write-up.

`grounded-tasks.txt` is the same prompts under task boundaries and is the natural
third row, but the fold is what it exercises and the fold does not fire at 96K —
so it is the 8192 half's companion, not a third window.

## What it costs

- **~24 GB of pulls** and a second tokenizer on disk.
- **A reboot of the BC-250**, revertible; and, if the firmware knob is ever
  touched, one that is not.
- **The BC-250's numbers stop being comparable across the change.** Moving the
  split changes the machine, so `the-bc250-run`'s figures describe the old
  configuration and the inventory row needs both, not a replacement.
- **No recording made before this is comparable on tokens**, because Qwen3.8's
  tokenizer is not Qwen2.5-Coder's. The design already forbids continuing one
  history under two rulers; this is the same rule at the corpus level.
- **Nothing in the tree changes.** `--backend openai --openai-url` has reached a
  `llama-server` since
  [`an-openai-compatible-backend`](2026-09-01.an-openai-compatible-backend.completed.md).
  The GPU is a URL, and the packing is the serving stack's business.

## What was rejected, and why

- **Editing `luu-design.md`'s "32b fits in 48 GB and not in 16" now.** It is
  about to be wrong and it is not wrong *yet*, because no measurement this
  project holds says otherwise. It is rewritten when run 3 says so, with a record
  behind it — which is the order this repo exists to keep.
- **Citing the article's table as evidence.** Fourth obligation of `local-first`.
  It is a claim with a named author and a named card; that is all it is here.
- **Running the same corpus on both machines to compare them.** [`machines.md`](../ROADMAP/2026-09-09/machines.md):
  one machine, one question nothing else can answer. Run 1–2 ask *how much memory
  can this board be given*, run 3–5 ask *what does a 27B change*. Neither is the
  other's control.
- **Re-litigating TurboQuant.** `the-14b-context-ceiling` rejected it on the
  maintainer's own reasoning and that reasoning still holds — it quantizes the KV
  cache. This is a different lever on a different term, and the earlier rejection
  is not evidence either way about this one.
- **Blaming `the-bc250-run` for the carve-out sentence.** The numbers in it are
  right and its correction to the inventory was right. Only the word
  *board-level* was doing work it had not earned.
- **Chasing the article's agent, its browser extension, or its harness.** Out of
  scope. One posture note is worth keeping, though, because it is the inverse of
  this project's: the article reports its agent failing on *"a sandbox restriction
  that I could have removed."* Here the restriction is the feature — the approved
  plan **is** the sandbox.

## Still open

- **Whether the packing claim survives contact with a corpus.** The article's
  *near lossless* is the author's impression plus a story-writing benchmark.
  Run 4 is the first time it meets a key on disk.
- **Whether the firmware carve-out is reachable on this board at all.** Not
  attempted, deliberately, until the GTT knob has been priced.
- **Whether `ggml-vulkan` will place tensors in GTT.** The pivot the whole BC-250
  half rests on, unknown, and cheap to find out.
- **KV quantization quality on the *new* model.** The 2026-09-15 section of
  `the-14b-context-ceiling` closed this for the 14B. A different architecture —
  hybrid, with SSM layers — has no claim to that answer, and the article's two
  profiles differ in KV type as well as in MTP, so its own numbers do not
  separate them either.
- **Parallel slots.** `--parallel 2 --no-kv-unified` gives a slot per session. A
  run with two sessions is not comparable on prefix reuse to a single-slot one,
  and prefix reuse is the number most of this repo's context work is scored on.
  Untouched here; it belongs to whatever record first wants two agents.
- **Qwen3.8 thinks by default.** Reasoning tokens spend the completion budget,
  not the prompt, but they move every wall-clock figure against the
  `qwen2.5-coder` runs. Nothing in this repo has ever scored seconds, so this is
  a note for whoever reads a slow run, not a measurement to take.
