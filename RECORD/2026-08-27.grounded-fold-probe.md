# The grounded fold probe: what to run, and how to read it

**Provenance.** Written on `claude/continuamos-cozerj`, an unmerged branch that
implemented tasks in parallel with the branch that became PR #5. Ported here with
the fragment surface it depends on, and adapted where the two trees differ: the
scripts are this tree's `##` directives, and this tree's scripts carry their
approved plan rather than asking the model for one, so the branch's argument
about a planning call seeing the pending fragment does not apply to a scripted
run. Everything else is as it was written.

**Status:** a protocol, and nothing else. Everything in here is for a machine
with a model on it; none of it has been run. It exists because the question the
whole task boundary is judged by has been "still open" in four consecutive
records, and the reason it stayed open was never the model — it was that nothing
in the corpus was grounded. That is fixed, so the question is now askable and
this is how to ask it.

## The question, stated so that it can come out wrong

> When a task closes, its turns stop being sent verbatim and its deterministic
> summary stands in for them. **Does a later turn still get a right answer to
> something only those turns could tell it?**

Two failure modes worth telling apart, because they want different fixes:

- **The summary dropped it.** The evidence block records paths, commands, exit
  lines and denials — not what was *learned*. A turn that answered from an
  attached file leaves nothing in the summary about what the file said.
- **The summary kept it and the model ignored it.** Different problem, different
  fix (prompt shape, or where the summary sits in the window), and confusing the
  two is how a compaction strategy gets blamed for a rendering bug.

## What is already known, so it is not re-measured

Against the mock (`--mock-delay-ms 0 --context-limit 8192 --reserve 512`):

- Both scripts attach the same fragments at the same turns — 205, 230 and 285
  tokens of `code` at turns 1, 6 and 11, nothing after — and `diff` on the two
  with directives stripped is empty. They are a comparison.
- Folding and reuse numbers at two windows are in
  [`2026-08-30.tasks-in-code.md`](2026-08-30.tasks-in-code.md) and
  [`2026-08-27.the-m4-pro-run.md`](2026-08-27.the-m4-pro-run.md). **None of that
  is what this probe is for.** The mock's answers are canned; it can say nothing
  about whether an answer survived.

## The runs

Exactly the recipe in `AGENTS.md`, pointed at the grounded pair. `OLLAMA_KEEP_ALIVE`
must be set **before** `ollama serve` starts, and `ollama ps` should show the
model resident before *and* between the two runs — a reload between the halves
makes the second one cold and the comparison a measurement of that.

```sh
for script in grounded grounded-tasks; do
  luu chat --script scripts/tasks/$script.txt \
    --backend ollama --model qwen2.5-coder:7b \
    --context-limit 8192 --reserve 512 \
    --tokenizer ~/models/qwen2.5-coder-7b/tokenizer.json \
    --record ~/records/$script.jsonl
done
```

Both runs must use the same model, the same window, the same tokenizer and the
same working tree — the fragments are read from disk at run time, so editing
`context.rs` between the halves silently changes the input.

### Precondition, and the honest fallback

**Sampling is not pinned.** The Ollama request carries `num_ctx` and nothing
else, so temperature and seed are the server's defaults and the two runs differ by
the sampler as well as by the fold. Two ways out:

1. **Pin it** — `options.temperature = 0` and a fixed `options.seed`, which is
   about fifteen lines in `ollama.rs` and two flags. Then a difference between
   the runs is the fold or nothing. This is the better probe and it is not built.
2. **Run each side twice** and treat only differences that repeat as real. Costs
   two more runs and no code, and it is enough to catch a difference that is
   worth acting on.

Do not skip both. One run each, unpinned, cannot distinguish "the summary lost
it" from "the sampler wandered", and that is the entire finding.

## What to read, and where the answers are

The last five turns of each run — turns 16–20, the group that attaches nothing.
Read them side by side. What each one is asking for:

| turn | asks about | a right answer contains | strength |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | `context.rs:1-15`, turn 1 | *decide then render*, and *the stable prefix stays byte-identical* | **strong** — the wording is this repo's |
| 17 | `task.rs:1-18`, turn 6 | the cut point compaction takes, the scope permission is granted at, and the grouping the transcript shows — **three jobs, one boundary** | **strong** |
| 18 | `luu.toml:1-26`, turn 11 | `cargo`, `rustc`, `git`, `rg`, `ls` | **strongest** — five specific strings, either there or not |
| 19 | `luu.toml:1-26`, turn 11 | on macOS or a kernel without Landlock, `run_command` is denied and says what is missing | medium — a model could guess "it fails" |
| 20 | itself | which of the four it read and which it filled in | not a probe — it is the model's self-report, and a model's account of its own work is not evidence. Read it, score nothing by it |

Turn 18 is the one to trust most. Five literal strings, no paraphrase, and a
model that never saw `luu.toml` has no way to produce `rg` in that list.

In the **plain** run those three files are still in the window (at 8192 nothing
evicted in the earlier session — check the `history` bucket to confirm it for
this one). In the **tasks** run they were folded into summaries at turns 5, 10
and 15. So a right answer in plain and a wrong one in tasks, on the same turn,
with sampling controlled, is the fold losing it.

Beside it, per run, two numbers already in the recording: total tokens sent
(every model call a turn makes, tool round trips included) and mean reuse over
all calls. A fold that loses an answer is not paid for by any token saving; a
fold that keeps every answer and costs less is the result this design has been
arguing for since the modes record.

**Also read the summaries themselves.** They are in the recording as
`task_closed`, and if turn 18 fails in the tasks run, the summary for task 3 is
the place to see why: it will name `luu.toml` as a file the plan declared and say
nothing about what was in it. That is the deterministic summary working exactly
as specified, and the specification being wrong.

## What to write down

A new dated record: the table, the four answers verbatim from each run (short —
one line each is enough), the two numbers, and the verdict. If the verdict is
that folding loses answers, `loude-design.md`'s Tasks section changes with it,
and the next entry is a proposal for what the summary should carry — the obvious
candidate being *what the attached fragments were and one line of what they
said*, which is the first thing in this design that would put model prose into
the write-once region, and therefore wants its own argument rather than a patch.

## What this cannot answer, whatever it says

- **One model, one window, one script.** A 7B at 8192 on twenty turns. Nothing
  here generalises to 32B at 32K, and the M4 Pro run already showed the window
  flipping a conclusion.
- **Four questions is not a sample.** It is a probe: it can show a failure, it
  cannot show its absence.
- **The judge stays out of it.** Shadow mode needs about thirty closed tasks
  before its accuracy means anything, and this run produces four. The labels
  here are a person's, which is the only authority the design gives anyone.
