# What an unapproved turn may reach

Rows 31 and 32 of [`ROADMAP/2026-09-21`](../ROADMAP/2026-09-21/), and they are
one question asked twice: **the floor exists and nothing says what it is.** Row
31 is the person at the gate, row 32 is the reader of a recording. Both were
born in [`the-drafts-floor`](2026-09-21.the-drafts-floor.completed.md) §Still
open, the day the floor landed.

The roadmap puts them in one neighbourhood with row 33 and says why: the
alternation and the floor both landed as changes to *what runs*, and neither
reached a surface anybody watches.

## Where it stands

`Agency::new` derives the floor once — `sandbox.read_only()`, every explicit
root downgraded to `Access::Read`, commands, network, egress, enforcement and
limits untouched — and `serve` falls back to it for any turn no approved plan
holds. Two things follow and neither is visible:

- **The gate panel shows what a plan asks for and never what the turn in front
  of it may already do.** Since
  [`the-gate-panel-narrows`](2026-09-17.the-gate-panel-narrows.completed.md) it
  shows reads, writes, commands, network, egress and enforcement — all of them
  the *proposal*. A person approving is deciding what to **add**, and the panel
  states one of the two terms.
- **A refusal names the floor and not the draft.** `Authority::Plan(job)` prints
  *the approved plan for job 7*; `Authority::Draft` prints *the draft's floor,
  which grants no writes*. The reader is left to join the refusal's turn to
  `TurnStarted.job` to find out which draft was refused, on the one arm where
  the authority does not denormalise it.

## Row 31: send the floor, do not re-derive it

`Settings` gains a `floor`, filled at `get_settings` from the live agency beside
`posture` and `base`, which are there for the same reason — a fact about the
*session*, read at the route rather than carried from the destination resolved
at startup:

```rust
struct Floor {
    /// What an unapproved turn may read, as the policy declares it.
    reads: Vec<String>,
    /// What it may run. The session's, unchanged: subtracting the allowlist
    /// would take `rg` and `ls`, which is what exploration is for.
    commands: Vec<String>,
    /// Whether it may reach the network. The session's, unchanged.
    network: bool,
}
```

**There is no `writes` field and that is the shape of the claim.** The floor
grants none by construction, so a field that is always empty would invite a
reader to wonder when it is not. The panel says *writes: nothing* as a
sentence, from the absence.

**Derived in Rust and sent, rather than computed in the page.** The page has
`posture.network` and could almost spell the rest; `Sandbox::read_only` in
JavaScript would be the second implementation of a policy decision, which is the
failure `Agency`'s own doc comment names — *two call sites resolving a policy
differently is two sandboxes* — and which item 26 found six literals of. A page
that re-derives the floor is a page that can disagree with the sandbox about
what a turn may do, at the one moment somebody is deciding.

**Rendered relative to the base where it is under it.** `to_policy()` returns
canonical absolute paths; the person reading the gate wrote `.` in a policy
file. Display only — nothing is ever addressed by what this line shows, which is
the standing rule for `base` on the same struct.

## Row 32: the draft is nameable one turn in, and `Option` is the honest shape

[`the-drafts-floor`](2026-09-21.the-drafts-floor.completed.md) wrote this on the
variant, and it is right:

> **It carries no [`JobId`] on purpose.** The floor is derived from the session
> and not from the job, and a draft's id does not exist when the sandbox is
> chosen — `Context::open_draft` runs when a turn lands, so on the first prompt
> of a session there is no draft to name yet.

**This proposal contradicts the conclusion and keeps the reason.** The reason
covers exactly one turn — the first prompt of a session, where `live_task()` is
`None` — and not the turns after it, where a draft is open and its id is sitting
in the same `session.context` the fallback arm already reads. So the variant
becomes `Draft(Option<JobId>)`: `None` where the record's sentence applies, and
the draft's id everywhere else.

The stamp is the builder that already exists and is what the plan arm does:

```rust
// today
_ => agency.floor().clone(),

// proposed
(live, _) => match live.filter(|job| session.context.job(*job).is_some_and(Job::is_draft)) {
    Some(draft) => Arc::new(agency.floor().as_ref().clone().under(Authority::Draft(Some(draft)))),
    None => agency.floor().clone(),
},
```

**Only when the live job is a draft.** A live job that *has* a plan and reaches
this arm anyway is the widening item 26 closed; naming it a draft would be the
panel and the recording agreeing on something false. It keeps `None` there and
the text stays what it is today, which is already what that case prints.

### What it costs

- **A `Sandbox` clone per drafting turn** instead of an `Arc` clone: two `Vec`s
  and a `PathBuf` each. Beside a model call it is noise, and it buys the same
  denormalisation the plan arm has had since the gate existed.
- **`Authority` changes shape on the worker IPC**, from a unit variant to a
  newtype. Host and worker are the same binary, so that is not a compatibility
  surface — `the-drafts-floor` §4 established exactly this for the variant's
  introduction, and it does not reach `record::FORMAT` or `protocol::VERSION`
  because a denial carries the authority as *formatted text* in `Verdict::rule`.
- **The words in a refusal change again**, one day after they last changed. That
  is the visible half and it is the point: *the draft's floor, which grants no
  writes* becomes *the floor for draft 3, which grants no writes*.

## What was rejected

- **A `writes: []` field**, above: an always-empty list is a question mark where
  a sentence belongs.
- **Computing the floor in `store.js`** from `posture` plus the sandbox string.
  The sandbox is a formatted blob on the wire; parsing it in a browser would
  make the page's idea of the floor a function of a display format.
- **Leaving row 32 to the join.** `TurnStarted.job` plus `ToolResult.turn` does
  answer *which draft*, and the asymmetry is the argument: one arm of the same
  enum denormalises it and the other does not, so a reader who knows the plan
  arm learns the wrong lesson from the draft one.
- **A second panel for the session's sandbox.** The settings modal already
  prints `agency.describe()`. The floor belongs where the decision is, and a
  person at the gate is not going to open Settings to find the other term.

## Still open

- **The floor is a session fact and the panel only shows it at the gate.** Most
  drafting turns happen with no gate up, and nothing says what they may reach
  while they run. Row 33's neighbourhood — what the page does with an
  alternation — and deliberately not this file's.
- **Nothing measures how often the floor actually refuses anything**, which is
  row 29's denominator problem one surface along.

## Appended 2026-09-21, second section: built, and the three things the argument had not seen

Both rows landed as proposed. What follows is what building them changed or
found.

### 1. The floor is chosen twice, and only one of the two had been read

§Row 32 quoted one site — the turn's sandbox in `start_turn` — and there is a
second, in `begin_turn`, where the *selector* reads spans through the sandbox
the turn will run under. Its own comment says why it is there:

> A no-op for what is *selected* — the floor differs from the policy file only
> in the write bit, and selecting reads — but the two arms of one decision
> written twice is how they drift.

Stamping one and not the other would have made a selection denial say *the
draft's floor* while a tool denial in the same turn said *the floor for draft
3*. So the stamp is a function both call, `floor_holding`, and the comment's own
warning is what found the second site. **The two sites still differ in how they
decide** — `begin_turn` trusts `session.narrowed` alone, `start_turn` compares
it against the live job — and unifying *that* is a change to what holds a turn,
which is not this record's to make.

### 2. `Draft(None)` is not dead code, and the first prompt is not the only way to reach it

The `None` arm covers two cases and the argument named one. The other is the
guard: a live job that **has a plan** and reaches the floor anyway. That is the
widening item 26 closed, so it should never happen — and it is exactly the case
where naming the job would make a recording confidently wrong about which kind
of job ran unapproved. It keeps the unstamped words, which is what that case
prints today, and the filter that does it is one `is_draft` call.

### 3. The wire needed two shapes tested, not one

`Authority` is adjacently tagged, and the round-trip test had one `Draft`. A
unit variant becomes a newtype variant here, so `Draft(None)` serializes with
`null` in the content field — the case a `skip_serializing_if` would have
quietly turned into a missing key, which is the same class of bug
`record::Posture::name` has a comment about. Both arms are in the test now.

### What was run

- `cargo test --workspace`: **517 passed, 0 failed** (515 before these rows).
- `cargo fmt --all --check` and `RUSTFLAGS=-Dwarnings cargo clippy --workspace
  --all-targets`: clean.
- The 16 live-server Playwright specs against a release `luu serve`: **all
  green**, including the gate spec's new assertion.

Each of the three new behaviours was confirmed against its absence:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The stamp** | Reverted to `agency.floor().clone()`: the refusal reads *"/tmp (read) does not grant read-write on … under the draft's floor, which grants no writes"* and the test fails on the missing name |
| **The wire field** | `settings.floor` removed at the route: *the floor is the other term of the gate's question* |
| **The panel line** | `p.floor` deleted from `app.html` and the gate spec run against a **debug** binary, where `rust-embed` reads the page from disk: the spec fails. Restored: green |

That last one is worth a line of its own. `rust-embed` bakes the page into a
release binary and reads it from disk in debug, so a browser test can be pointed
at `target/debug/luu` to check a page change against its absence without a
two-minute rebuild. Nothing in the suite says so.

## Still open, after building it

- **The two sites decide differently**, §1: one trusts the narrowing, the other
  checks it against the live job. Both mean *the plan when there is one*, and
  one of them would not notice a narrowing left behind by a job that is no
  longer live.
- **The floor is only shown at the gate.** Most drafting turns happen with no
  gate up. Row 33's neighbourhood, unchanged by this.
- **`Floor` is a third description of a sandbox on the wire**, beside
  `Settings::sandbox` (a formatted blob) and `record::Posture` (three facts). A
  fourth would be the argument for one.
