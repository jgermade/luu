# What leaves the history: the window is 91% quoted code

**Status:** a proposal, and the code it plans. Roadmap item 6 —
[*active pruning of tool results*](../ROADMAP/2026-09-05/README.md) — which has
been ordered without an argument since it was written, plus the open thread
[`selection-at-the-gate`](2026-09-06.selection-at-the-gate.completed.md) left
behind that morning. They are the same mechanism and this file merges them,
which is the first thing it has to justify.

## The problem, in one row of the run that is already on disk

The three arms of [`does-the-model-read-it`](2026-09-06.does-the-model-read-it.completed.md),
at their last turn, from [`runs/2026-09-06.precision/cost.json`](runs/2026-09-06.precision/cost.json):

| arm | turn 38's window | history | code | evictions | mean reuse |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `off` | **turns 1–37** | 5 898 | 0 | 0 | 93.3% |
| `--map-tokens 1024` | turns 3–37 | 1 570 | 0 | 1 | 97.1% |
| `--select-tokens 1024` | **turns 33–37** | 5 859 | 1 096 | 31 | **17.7%** |

The two outer rows are the finding. **The same 8 192-token window holds either
thirty-seven turns of conversation or five turns of quoted code**, and the
selector chose the second without anybody deciding it should. History did not
grow; it was replaced.

Reconstructing what is inside that 5 859: the code bucket of each turn in the
window, once it becomes history, is **~89–91% of the history block** from turn
20 onwards (4 450 of 4 974 at turn 20; 5 322 of 5 859 at turn 38 — an estimate,
because the two are counted on either side of a message boundary and
tokenization is not additive across one). The bucket named `history` is, by
turn 20, not a record of a conversation. It is a copy of parts of the repository,
re-sent every turn, and 83% more prompt tokens over the run (254 273 against
138 651).

## Two mechanisms, one question

Both were written knowing this file would have to exist. They say so:

> Tool output is capped rather than pruned. Pruning old results out of the
> history is a later, measured change; a cap now is the part that is not a
> strategy — a `cat` of a 2 MB file must not be able to blow the window open.
> — [`tools/mod.rs:27`](../crates/agent-core/src/tools/mod.rs#L27), `MAX_OUTPUT_BYTES = 8 KiB`

> Rendered fused into the user message, stored apart so that pruning can reach
> it later without parsing back what we already wrote.
> — [`context.rs:146`](../crates/agent-core/src/context.rs#L146), `Turn::code_context`

An 8 KiB `cat` and a 1 000-token fragment are the same object to the window: a
block of file bytes that earned its place in **one** turn and is charged to every
turn afterwards until eviction throws away the conversation around it. Ordering
them as separate items would produce two rules for one question, and the question
is the project's own: *what earns its place in the prompt* — asked a second time,
of something that already earned it once.

## What this repository's corpus can say, and what it cannot

The 38 questions are independent by construction — one per file, chosen before
any of this — so they are the instrument least likely to show a repeat. They show
one anyway. Replaying the recorded selections against the recorded eviction
floors:

- **15 of 38 turns selected a span that was still visible in the window**, put
  there by an earlier turn.
- That is **~3 746 of 40 605 code tokens, 9.2%** (per-fragment tokens estimated
  by line share of the turn's `code` bucket).
- The clusters are exactly where a reader would put them: turn 4 repeats three of
  turn 3's spans (~595 tokens), turn 24 repeats four (~404), turn 16 repeats two
  (~503).

9.2% is the **floor**, measured on the corpus built to exclude the effect. What
it cannot say is what a conversation does, because it contains none — the point
`selection-at-the-gate` already made and the reason it put this out of scope.

## The proposal

### 1. The decision belongs at render, not at push

The obvious implementation — when selecting for turn *n*, drop what an earlier
turn already showed — is wrong, and the same replay says how wrong. Counting the
cases where a turn borrowed a span and the turn it borrowed from later fell below
the floor **while the borrower was still in the window**: **22**. Turn 4 borrows
three spans from turn 3; at turn 9 the floor passes 3 and turn 4 is left quoting
nothing, in a window that still contains it.

That is the [`the-fold-fix-verified`](2026-08-30.the-fold-fix-verified.completed.md)
failure with a different mechanism: a model reasoning over code it can no longer
see. Twenty-two chances to reproduce it, on the least favourable corpus.

So: `Turn::code_context` keeps everything, always. What changes is
[`Context::select`](../crates/agent-core/src/context.rs#L760) — the one place
that already knows which turns fit, because it just decided
([`context.rs:842`](../crates/agent-core/src/context.rs#L842) renders
`user_text(&turn.code_context, &turn.prompt)` for exactly the turns above the
floor). A span is skipped only when a turn **that is being rendered in this same
call** carries it. Eviction can then never orphan anything, because the two
decisions are one decision.

### 2. Two rules, and they are not the same size

| | what it drops | prefix cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Do not re-inject** | a span in this turn's `code` bucket that a rendered turn already shows | **none** — only the newest message changes, which is the one that changes anyway |
| **B. Prune behind** | the fragment or tool result inside an *older* rendered turn | rewrites the history block from that turn on — **once**, then stable |

A is prefix-safe and worth ~9.2% here. B is where the 91% lives, and it is the
same trade [`Eviction::Block`](../crates/agent-core/src/context.rs#L187) already
made and measured: a deep, infrequent rewrite beats a shallow one every turn.
They ship as two flags because they are two measurements — the rule this
repository has not broken yet.

### 3. What B replaces a pruned block with

Not nothing, and not a summary. `[read crates/agent-core/src/context.rs:453-464
— 312 tokens, dropped at turn 31]`: the path, the span, and that it went. Model
prose in the history is rejected for the reason the fold rejects it
([`job.rs:557`](../crates/agent-core/src/job.rs#L557)) — it would enter a region
every later turn is built on, where being wrong is unrecoverable.

### 4. The accounting has to follow, or the budget lies

`Turn::tokens` is counted once at push
([`context.rs:476`](../crates/agent-core/src/context.rs#L476)) and read back by
`item_tokens` → `fits_from`. A render that sends less than it counted evicts on
tokens nobody sent: the cut gets deeper than the window needs, which is the
exact failure this change exists to reduce. Either `tokens_of` learns what the
render will drop, or the count moves to where the decision is. This is the part
most likely to be got wrong quietly, and it is why the whole thing is one record
rather than a flag.

## The instrument, which nearly exists and is currently broken

`scripts/tasks/grounded.txt` is already conversation-shaped: twenty prompts in
**three groups of five about one file each**, then five that ask again with
nothing attached. Run with `--select-tokens` instead of its `## fragment:`
directives, it is the first corpus in this repository where consecutive turns
select overlapping spans — which is the whole case for A.

It does not run today. Both grounded scripts attach
`crates/agent-core/src/task.rs`, renamed to `job.rs` in
[`d70e6d9`](../crates/agent-core/src/job.rs), and a fragment that cannot be read
is an error by design, not a warning:

```
$ luu chat "x" --fragment crates/agent-core/src/task.rs:1-18
Error: fragment crates/agent-core/src/task.rs:1-18
Caused by: reading …/task.rs:1-18: No such file or directory
```

Five of twenty prompts in each script have been unrunnable since the rename, and
nothing noticed, because nothing has run them since. Fixing the path is a line;
it is written here because *the corpus rotted silently* is the same failure mode
as the stale coverage table `does-the-model-read-it` found, and the second
occurrence is the one that says it is a pattern.

## What this costs

- **Two more flags off by default**, and the comparability rule that keeps every
  recording made before them readable.
- **B breaks prefix reuse at the moment it fires.** The claim that it wins is a
  claim about frequency, not about size, and it is exactly the claim
  `Eviction::Block` had to prove separately.
- **A cannot pay on a corpus of independent questions**, so a run that shows 9.2%
  is not evidence for it — the conversation corpus is a precondition, not a
  follow-up.
- **The model may need the repetition.** Nothing here has measured whether a 7B
  attends to a span quoted eleven turns up as well as to one in the current
  message. Position inside the *bucket* does not matter
  (`does-the-model-read-it`: 13/14 held-but-not-first); position across the
  *window* is a different question and this record does not get to borrow that
  answer.

## What was rejected

- **Dropping fragments from the stored turn, keeping them only in the prompt.**
  Rejected in `selection-at-the-gate` and rejected again here, for the same
  measured reason: it is `the-fold-fix-verified`'s failure, one scope smaller.
- **Summarising a fragment or a tool result with the model.** Prose in the
  write-once region. The fold's deterministic summary is the standing answer.
- **Lowering `MAX_OUTPUT_BYTES`.** It caps one result; it cannot reach the
  thirty already in the window, and tuning it is a strategy chosen by a constant.
- **Deduplicating by content hash across the session.** It answers a question
  nobody asked — the same *bytes* from two different spans are not the same
  claim, and a path plus a span is what a person reading the record needs.

## Still open

- **Which of A and B, and in what order.** The record proposes both and does not
  claim to know; the honest sequence is A first, because it cannot break the
  prefix, and B behind a measurement that A makes possible.
- **Tool results and fragments may not want the same rule.** A `cat` is evidence
  a job produced; a fragment is context a turn was handed. The fold already
  treats them differently ([`job.rs:529`](../crates/agent-core/src/job.rs#L529)
  takes `steps` and `shown` separately) and this record assumes one rule covers
  both without arguing it.
- **What a pruned turn does to the fold**, which quotes `shown` verbatim at
  close. If B has already dropped a span, the fold must still find it — the
  bytes are on disk in `code_context`, so this is a *policy* choice about what
  the summary quotes, and it is not made here.
