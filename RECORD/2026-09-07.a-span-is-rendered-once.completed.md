# A span is rendered once, by the oldest turn that has it

**Status:** rule A of
[`what-leaves-the-history`](2026-09-06.what-leaves-the-history.completed.md), built,
measured, and off by default. That record **stays `WIP`**: it proposed two
flags and one of them exists.

Three things came back from the proposal changed, and one of them changes the
rule itself. This file is the argument for the version that shipped.

## 1. Skipping the newest bucket saves nothing

The proposal named the mechanism precisely:

> A span is skipped only when a turn **that is being rendered in this same
> call** carries it.

and then described it as a change to *this turn's* `code` bucket — "a span in
this turn's `code` bucket that a rendered turn already shows". Built that way
it pays for exactly one turn. `Turn::code_context` keeps everything (which the
proposal is right about and this does not touch), so the turn that skipped a
span is pushed carrying it, and the next call renders it as history —
[`context.rs:889`](../crates/agent-core/src/context.rs#L889) sends
`user_text(&turn.code_context, …)` for every turn above the floor. The saving
evaporates the moment the turn it was made in stops being the newest.

So the rule is not about the newest bucket. It is about **the render**: walking
the window from the floor forward, a span is written out by the first turn that
carries it and skipped by every turn after it, the turn being asked included.
That is the same sentence the proposal wrote; it is one scope wider than the
change it described.

## 2. Which end owns it is the whole of why rule A is cheap

The proposal does not say, and the two answers are different items.

| | who renders a shared span | what moves in the prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **oldest owns** | the first turn in the window that has it | the newest message only |
| **newest owns** | the turn being asked | the history, from that turn down, **every time a span repeats** |

Newest-owns is rule B's cost arriving under rule A's name — a deep rewrite of
the cached block, which `Eviction::Block` had to prove separately and which
this has not measured. Oldest-owns leaves everything above the newest message
byte-identical, which is the claim the proposal made for A and the reason it
put A first. It is also the safer half of the orphan question: the owner is
always the turn that will be evicted *first*, so ownership is being handed
forward, never backward.

`Repeat::{Always, Once}` on `Budget`, `--repeat-once` on `chat`, `serve` and
`stdio`. `Always` is the default and renders byte-for-byte what every
recording on disk was made under.

## 3. The accounting, which the proposal was right to flag

§4 of the proposal:

> Either `tokens_of` learns what the render will drop, or the count moves to
> where the decision is. This is the part most likely to be got wrong quietly.

It is, and it is wrong in **two** places rather than one.

- **A turn's cost.** The stored count less what this render will not send:
  subtraction, not a recount, so a turn that repeats nothing costs exactly what
  it always did and every number in every recording made before `Repeat`
  existed still reads as itself.
- **`Evicted.tokens`, which was already an over-report waiting for this.** It
  summed the items below the new floor. Under `Once` that is a saving the
  window does not get: a span the evicted turn *owned* does not leave with it,
  it passes to the next turn in the window that carries it. The number is now
  the difference of two windows — what the history cost before the cut, less
  what it costs after — which reduces to the old sum exactly when nothing is
  deduplicated, and is honest when something is.

The one place a fixed point would be needed is the current turn's own bucket:
what it gives up depends on where the floor lands, and the floor is decided
using that number. It is not solved, deliberately, because the direction is
safe — the cut is computed against the same budget it would have had without
the rule, so **the cut is never deeper than the default's**, and the prompt
that goes out is never larger than the one it was sized for. There is a test
that sweeps 70 window sizes for it, and it fails if the rule never once keeps
*more* history than the default: never-worse alone proves nothing.

## What it does, on the corpus that could not run until today

Twenty turns of `scripts/tasks/grounded.txt` at `--select-tokens 1024
--context-limit 8192`, one flag apart. Full numbers and the two recordings in
[`runs/2026-09-07.repeat-once/`](runs/2026-09-07.repeat-once/).

| | `off` | `--repeat-once` | |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| prompt tokens, 20 turns | 125 300 | **120 980** | −3.4% |
| `code` bucket | 20 274 | **16 318** | **−19.5%** |
| `history` bucket | 95 111 | 94 747 | −0.4% |
| cuts | 12 | **8** | |
| mean prefix reuse | 34.0% | **52.0%** | |

**The saving is in `code` and not in `history`, and that is the rule working
rather than a disappointment.** Oldest-owns means the history keeps what it
had; the turn being asked is the one that stops repeating. Read the two rows
together: 20 turns sent 3 956 fewer tokens of code and the history did not
grow to absorb them.

**−19.5% against the 9.2% the proposal's replay predicted.** The replay ran on
`map-order-probe` — 38 questions about 38 different files, built to exclude
repetition. This corpus asks five consecutive questions about one file. Both
numbers are right about their own corpus, and the distance between them is
what the proposal meant by *a conversation corpus is a precondition, not a
follow-up*.

**Prefix reuse rose 18 points, and nothing predicted that.** A cheaper turn
fills the window more slowly, so the same 13 turns leave it in 8 cuts instead
of 12, and a cut is what rewrites the history block from its front. This is
`Eviction::Block`'s argument arriving through a different door. It is one
corpus at one window size under one eviction policy, and it is a consequence
of the rule rather than a claim made for it.

**The backend is the mock.** No answer in that run was written by a model, so
nothing in it says the rule is free of accuracy cost. What is real is
everything the rule touches: the selector, the tree, the window, the buckets.

## What it costs

- **A flag that is off, and the comparability rule that keeps it that way.**
- **A `HashSet` of `&Fragment` across the render**, so `Fragment` is now an
  identity as well as a value. Identity is **path *and* bytes**: the same spec
  after an edit is a different claim, and treating it as the same one is
  precisely the bug [`resolving-a-symbol`](2026-09-07.resolving-a-symbol.completed.md)
  closed four commits ago — wrong lines under a right path, delivered as the
  definition.
- **Two counts of a fragment per select** where one turn repeats another's
  span, to subtract it. Bounded by the number of repeats, which is the thing
  being removed.
- **The model may need the repetition, and this still does not know.** The
  proposal said so and the mock run cannot answer it. Position inside the
  *bucket* does not matter (`does-the-model-read-it`: 13/14 held-but-not-first);
  position across the *window* — a span quoted eleven turns up against one in
  the current message — is a different question, and a 7B is where it gets
  asked.

## What was rejected

- **Newest-owns.** §2. It is rule B's rewrite under rule A's flag.
- **Choosing the owner over the whole session** rather than inside the window.
  It removes the circularity from the accounting and reintroduces
  `the-fold-fix-verified`: the owner can already be below the floor, and then
  nobody renders the span at all. The precision corpus offers that 22 chances.
- **Recounting a turn from its rendered text.** Exact, and quadratic over a
  session — the reason `Turn::tokens` is counted once at push and stored. The
  subtraction keeps every unaffected turn at the number it already had.
- **Deduplicating a span inside a folded job.** A fold sends its summary, not
  its turns, so nothing inside one is on the screen to stand in for anything.
  Folded items neither take ownership nor give it up, and there is a test.

## Still open

- **Rule B.** Untouched: the 91% lives there, and so does the prefix cost that
  makes it a separate measurement. `what-leaves-the-history` stays `WIP` for
  it.
- **Whether a 7B misses the repetition.** The corpus and the flag now exist,
  the model is on a machine, and this is the same one-flag-apart shape every
  other claim here has had to survive. Until it is run, the flag is off for
  a second reason as well as the first.
- **The current turn's bucket is not credited to the cut.** Stated above as
  safe rather than solved. It means `--repeat-once` evicts slightly earlier
  than a perfect implementation would; the error is bounded by what the turn
  being asked shares with the window, and it never goes the other way.
- **Tool results are still only capped.** The proposal's argument that an 8 KiB
  `cat` and a 1 000-token fragment are the same object to the window is
  untouched by this, and unimplemented: `Repeat` reaches `code_context` and not
  `ToolStep`.
