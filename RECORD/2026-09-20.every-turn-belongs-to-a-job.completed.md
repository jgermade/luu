# Every turn belongs to a job

**Status:** completed — the code it plans is written, and §fourth is what it
cost.

Item 22 of [`ROADMAP/2026-09-17`](../ROADMAP/2026-09-17/README.md), which that
revision calls *the largest item here and the one with the least code written
against it*. The shape was settled on 2026-09-18 in
[`the-window-rules-are-a-session-fact`](2026-09-18.the-window-rules-are-a-session-fact.completed.md)
§seventh and §eighth, across two appended sections written the same evening. This
file is what that shape turns out to cost once it is read against the code, and
it changes two of its parts.

## The shape, as it stands

```
session:
  job: draft   objective: what the user asked, as they asked it
               approve → close and fold, and open a plan
  job: plan    objective: the approved plan
               complete → close and fold, and open a draft
  job: draft   …
```

A session opens with a draft and jobs alternate. Approving *is* closing. The
negative decisions — `decline`, `more changes` — end nothing and keep you in the
job you are in, so there is no transition that produces two drafts in a row. At
most one job is open, everything behind it is a fold, and the window in steady
state is *N small summaries plus the open job's turns*.

Nothing in this file argues with that. What follows is the part §seventh and
§eighth could not see, because they were written about the window and the cost
lands somewhere else.

## The finding: `Turn::job` is not the `Option` that matters

§sixth's sentence is *`Turn::job` stops being `Option`*, and it is right about
the symptom. Read against the code, the optionality is not on the turn at all.

`Turn::job` is `Option<JobId>` and is set from `Context::live_job()` — *the last
job approved and not closed*. `Job` is not optional in any of its parts:

```rust
pub struct Job {
    pub id: JobId,
    pub objective: String,
    pub plan: Plan,          // ← this one
    pub state: JobState,
    …
}
```

**A draft is a job whose objective is known and whose plan is not.** That is the
whole of what a draft is, and `Plan` has no room to say it. So making `Turn::job`
total requires giving every draft a `Plan`, and the only `Plan` available to
write is the empty one.

**The empty plan is not "no restriction". It is the strictest plan there is.**
`Plan::writes`' own doc comment: *"A plan that declares none may not write at all
— the check is worth having only if the answer can be no."* A draft carrying
`Plan::default()` and narrowed through `Plan::narrow` produces a sandbox that
allows nothing, and every exploratory turn in the session — which is most turns
in most sessions — would be refused its first `read_file`.

**This does not happen today, and the reason it does not is a near miss.** The
gate is keyed on `Session::narrowed: Option<(JobId, Arc<Sandbox>)>`, which is set
at approval and at reopen and nowhere else. A turn with no job runs under the
session's own sandbox because `narrowed` is `None`, not because `turn.job` is
`None`. The two are the same today and item 22 is precisely the change that
separates them.

So the invariant this item has to hold, stated before anything is built:

> **A draft is a job for the window and is not a job for the gate.** Item 22 is a
> change to how the history folds. Nothing in it may change what the sandbox
> allows. A commit that makes a turn's sandbox a function of `turn.job` has
> smuggled a security change inside a context-management one.

## The proposal: the `Option` moves rather than disappearing

**`Job::plan` becomes `Option<Plan>`, and `Turn::job` becomes `JobId`.**

`None` is a draft: the job exists, it holds turns, it folds at approval, and
there is no plan because nobody has written one yet. The gate keeps reading
`narrowed`, which a draft never sets, so the sandbox diff at the end of this work
is **nothing** — which is the property that makes it reviewable.

Three things follow, and they are the argument:

**It says what a draft is, rather than encoding it.** A job with an empty plan
and a job with no plan are different claims about the same bytes, and the first
one is a claim `Plan::narrow` will act on. `Option` here is not a convenience; it
is the distinction.

**The turn gets what §sixth actually wanted.** *Which job was this turn asked
inside* becomes a total function from the session's first turn, with no approval
needed to reach one — which is the whole benefit §eighth names, and it arrives
without the gate having to know.

**It is one `Option` and not two.** Today a reader answering *what could this turn
reach* has to check `turn.job`, then find the job, then decide whether `state ==
Approved` means its plan applies. After this there is one question — *does this
job have a plan* — and the answer is a field.

### `JobState` collapses, and `Proposed` turns out to belong somewhere else

Today: `Proposed`, `Approved`, `Closed`, `Rejected`, with `is_open()` defined as
`state == Approved`. Under the alternation a draft is **open without being
approved**, so `is_open` as written would make every draft closed, every draft's
turns unattributed, and the change a no-op with extra fields. `is_open` has to
become *opened and not closed*.

Then the other two stop being job states, and §seventh already moved one of them:

- **`Rejected` → a decision event inside the draft.** §seventh's words, and its
  reason survives the move: *how many rounds a plan takes before it is approved
  is a number worth having, and a lifecycle where decline is silent cannot
  produce it.*
- **`Proposed` → the same, and §seventh did not say so.** A plan is proposed
  while its draft is still open; approval is what opens the plan job. So a
  proposed-and-not-yet-approved plan is not a job that exists in a state, it is a
  **thing offered inside the draft**, exactly like the decline that may answer
  it. `JobState` is then `Open` and `Closed`, which with `ClosedBy` beside it is
  the whole lifecycle.

That retires the reason `Rejected` was kept — *ids are handed out by position, so
removing one would make the next collide with it*. Under this, a rejected plan
never gets an id, because an id is what approval hands out.

### The zero-turn draft and the reopen are the same question

§eighth left one open and the code raises another, and one rule answers both.

**§eighth's:** complete a plan, approve the next one immediately, and the draft
between them closes with nothing in it — two messages of noise in the window.

**The code's:** `Context::reopen_job` takes any closed job and makes it live
again. In an alternation that is ambiguous the moment anything has happened
since: reopening job 2 while jobs 3 and 4 exist gives you two open jobs, or
obliges the reopen to close them, and neither is a thing anyone asked for.

**The rule: a job opens when a turn lands in it, not when the previous one
closes.** The session's current job is derived — *the last job not closed, or a
new draft if the last one is* — the way `Current` is derived one module along and
for the same reason: a fact computed from what the session holds cannot drift
from it.

Both fall out. A draft with no turns never gets an id, so it cannot fold to an
empty summary. And `reopen` is unambiguous exactly when nothing has been asked
since the close, which is now the only case in which there is nothing after it to
conflict with. Two open questions, one derivation, no stored field.

## What it costs

**`protocol::VERSION` and `record::FORMAT` both move**, and this is the first
item in this neighbourhood where the protocol bump is the larger half. A draft
opening, a plan being offered inside it, and a decline are three things a client
has to see in order to draw the gate, and none of them exists today —
`job_proposed` and `job_rejected` are the closest and they are about a job that
under this shape is not one.

**The wire keeps the `Option` and the model does not.** A recording from August
has turns with no job, and `SessionView::from_record` must not invent one.
Absent means *written before this*, which is not the same claim as *this turn was
in a draft* — the sentence format 11 was bumped under for the resend rules, and
the one format 15 needed last for `diverged`. So `TurnView::job` stays
`Option<JobId>` while `Turn::job` becomes total, and the conversion is where the
claim is made or refused.

**Every tally over jobs changes meaning.** `Counts::jobs` carries `proposed`,
`approved`, `rejected`, `closed`. Under the alternation every session closes
roughly twice as many jobs as it used to, because half of them are drafts, and
`proposed` and `rejected` stop counting jobs at all. A reader comparing a
September session against an October one will find the numbers doubling and
conclude the sessions got busier. That is the same failure `Supersession`'s doc
comment was written to head off a day earlier, one structure along, and it wants
the same treatment: the count says which shape it is counting, or the format
number does.

**`summary_text` is asked a question it has never been asked.** It builds a
fold's evidence from the stored steps of work that ran. A draft's turns ran no
tools, changed nothing and concluded something — *this is what we looked at, this
is what we decided* — and summarising that is a different job from summarising a
diff. Not hard; unwritten, and it is the part a reader of the folded window
actually reads.

**The largest cost is that it touches the gate's surface without changing the
gate.** Every route, every panel and every test that says *approve* now sits at
the boundary between two jobs rather than at the start of one. That is a lot of
call sites for a change whose whole point is that the sandbox diff is empty, and
it is why the invariant above is stated as a sentence somebody can review against.

## What is rejected

**A `JobKind` with more than two variants.** Draft and plan is the alternation;
a third kind is a second lifecycle wearing the same struct. If something is
neither, it is not a job.

**Nested jobs**, still. `live_job` is *one level, deliberately* and that is not
reopened here. An alternation of two kinds at one level is the whole model.

**Giving the draft the session's policy as its plan.** The tempting version of
the finding above: instead of `Option<Plan>`, write the session's own floor into
the draft as a real plan, so `Plan::narrow` always has something to narrow and
the gate panel can show what an exploratory turn may reach — which today it
cannot, and which item 12's field would like. Rejected **here** and not in
general. It is a change to what the sandbox is built from, arriving inside a
change to how the window folds, and the two would land in one commit with one
reviewer. If the gate should show a session's floor as a plan, that is its own
row and its own afternoon.

**Making the current job a stored field.** It is derivable from the jobs and the
turns, and the two would disagree the first time a resume rebuilt one and not the
other. `SessionView::counts()`'s argument, taken as read.

## Still open

- **What the model is told.** A draft's turns are the exploratory ones, and
  nothing in the system prompt or the objective says so. Whether a model behaves
  differently when it knows it is drafting rather than executing is unmeasured
  and is probably a real effect; it is also the kind of claim this project does
  not get to make without a run.
- **What the debug UI does with an alternation.** The gate panel approves a
  proposal; under this it closes a draft and opens a plan in one action, and the
  folded history it sits above is twice as long and half as uniform. Unargued.
- **Whether a draft's fold is worth its summary.** §eighth's *two messages of
  noise* was about a zero-turn draft and the rule above kills that case. A
  three-turn draft that concluded nothing is the same objection with a smaller
  number, and nothing here says where the floor is.
- **Where a resume lands.** A session resumed mid-draft has to come back into
  that draft, which is the first time `from_view` has had to reconstruct
  something derived rather than stored. Item 20's neighbourhood, and it is the
  one part of this that already has a shape to copy.

## Appended 2026-09-20, second section: what opens the gate, which neither §seventh nor §eighth wrote down

The shape above is settled and this section does not touch it. What it answers is
the question that appears the moment somebody tries to build it, and that three
records have now walked past: **under an alternation there is always an open job,
so `live_job().is_none()` — today's trigger for the planning call — is never true
again.** The gate has no door.

Read against `serve.rs`, today's flow is:

```
prompt, nothing open → planning call → job_proposed → held prompt waits
approve              → the held prompt runs under the approved plan
decline              → the held prompt is dropped on the floor
```

**So today no turn in a served session ever runs unapproved.** `Turn::job` is
`Option` in the type and is effectively total in practice, which is worth saying
plainly because it changes what the strict reading of §eighth costs.

### The strict reading, and why it is not this commit

§eighth's *a session always opens with a draft* read literally means the first
prompt runs exploratory and the gate fires later, on somebody's say-so. The
sandbox diff really is nothing — a draft never sets `narrowed`, so a draft turn
runs under the session's own policy file, exactly as a jobless turn does today.

But *what runs* is not nothing, and this project's own policy file is the
counter-example. `luu.toml` grants `.` at `read-write` and `cargo`, `rustc`,
`git`, `rg`, `ls`. A draft turn under it may **edit the repository and run the
build with nobody having approved anything** — where today the same prompt waits
behind the gate. That is not a change to what the sandbox allows; it is a change
to what reaches the sandbox, and it lands in a commit whose whole claim is that
the gate does not move. §The finding's invariant is written against one half of
that and the other half deserves it too:

> A draft is a job for the window and is not a job for the gate. **Nothing in
> item 22 may change what runs without a person deciding**, any more than it may
> change what the sandbox allows.

So the strict reading is rejected *here* and not in general, on the same grounds
and in the same words as §What is rejected's *giving the draft the session's
policy as its plan*: it is its own row and its own afternoon, and it wants a
measurement — how much of a session is exploration — that nothing has taken.

### What is built instead: the proposal stops having an id

Keep the trigger where it is, and the alternation falls out anyway once the
proposal stops pretending to be a job:

```
prompt, draft has no turns → planning call → plan_proposed { objective, plan, source }
                                             ← no id: there is no job yet
decline                    → plan_declined, and the held prompt RUNS
                                             ← the draft's first turn, so the draft opens here, as job 1
approve                    → job_approved { job: 2, from: null }
                                             ← the id is what approval hands out
```

This is not a compromise between the two readings; it is the only arrangement in
which all three of this file's rules hold at once, and it took building to see:

- **§The proposal's *an id is what approval hands out*.** A refused plan never
  gets one, because the thing refused was never addressed by one.
- **§The zero-turn draft's *a job opens when a turn lands in it*.** On the happy
  path — propose, approve, work — the draft never receives a turn, so it never
  opens and never folds to an empty summary. `from` is `null` on that approval
  and names the draft on the other one, which is the difference a reader wants.
- **The invariant above.** A turn runs exactly when it ran before. A decline now
  runs the prompt it used to drop, which is §seventh's own sentence — *decline
  and more changes keep you in the job you are in, nothing closes, the
  conversation continues* — and is the only behaviour in this commit that a user
  can observe changing.

**What it costs, said rather than discovered:** a session that approves
everything has drafts that never open, so its window is the plans alone and the
alternation is a shape it never takes. That is the honest state of the thing —
the drafts are reachable, they hold the turns a decline produces, and the day the
row above is taken they start holding the rest. A reader comparing this against
§eighth's diagram should read the diagram as where the shape is going and this as
how far the code has been taken.

**And what it means for the counts** is one more reason `Jobs` has to say which
shape it counts: `proposed` and `rejected` stop being job states here, but the
numbers behind them do not disappear — they become rounds inside a draft, which
is what §seventh kept `Rejected` for in the first place.

## Appended 2026-09-20, third section: the proposal's author moves the trigger, and the plan turns out to be the draft's compaction

§second kept today's trigger and built the gate around a proposal with no id. The
first half of that is now withdrawn by the person who asked for the item, in two
sentences that are worth quoting because the second one reframes what a plan *is*:

> Quizá jugaría con que el modelo sugiera un plan, dejando una opción explícita
> para pedir un plan.
>
> Entiendo que el plan es algo como la compactación del `job:draft`, descartando
> lo que se haya decidido en el refinamiento que viene siendo el propio draft.

### What the second sentence changes

§seventh already said *the draft's summary is the plan's context*. This says
something stronger and better: **the plan is what the draft compacts to.** The
draft is the refinement — the rounds, the reading, the things tried and dropped —
and the plan is the one artefact that survives it. That is not a restatement of
the fold; it is what the planning call should be *reading*.

Today's planning call reads a **held prompt** and answers before anything has
been explored, which is why it is a 7B's worst case: it is asked to plan work it
has not looked at. Under this it reads **the draft**, which is the window it just
spent N turns filling. The call does not move; what it is asked over does, and it
becomes the one call in the system that has the most context rather than the
least. Nothing here measures that and this record does not get to claim it — but
it is the first version of the planning call that could plausibly be worth a
run, and that run is now a row rather than an aspiration.

### What the first sentence changes: two doors, and neither is automatic

The gate gets **two** ways to open, and the automatic one is retired:

- **The model suggests.** An ordinary draft turn whose answer carries a plan
  block becomes a proposal. Nothing is approved by it and nothing runs under it;
  a person still decides, so this is a suggestion reaching the gate, not the
  gate opening itself.
- **A person asks.** `request_plan` runs the planning call over the draft as it
  stands. This is the explicit option, and it is what makes the first bullet
  optional rather than the only route.

And **no prompt is ever held again.** A prompt runs, in the draft, always — which
is what §eighth said all along and what §second could not give it.

### The cost §second refused, now unavoidable and stated plainly

§second rejected the strict reading because of what it does to *what runs*, and
that objection does not go away by being overruled — it goes into the open:

> **A draft turn runs under the session's own policy file, and on this
> repository that file grants `.` at `read-write` plus `cargo`, `rustc`, `git`.
> So an exploratory turn may edit the tree and run the build with nobody having
> approved anything.** Before item 22, the first prompt of a session waited
> behind the gate and this was unreachable.

The sandbox diff is still nothing — a draft sets no narrowing and `Plan::narrow`
is not called for one — and the invariant as written holds. What moved is what
reaches the unnarrowed sandbox, and it moved because the person whose repository
it is asked for it, after being shown the grant. It is recorded here rather than
in a commit message because the next person to read `luu.toml` and wonder when
exploration started being able to write deserves to find the sentence.

**The row it wants** is the one §What is rejected already named and declined to
take here: *giving the draft a plan of its own* — the session's floor narrowed to
reads, so exploration is exploration and the gate is what unlocks the write bit.
That is a change to what the sandbox is built from, it is one afternoon, and it
is the obvious next item after this one lands.

### Two smaller decisions this forces, both stated because neither is obvious

**A prompt arriving while a plan is on the table declines it.** *Decline* and
*more changes* are the same answer, and *more changes* said out loud is a person
typing the change they want. Refusing the prompt instead would be the `Pending`
refusal surviving into a shape that no longer holds a prompt to protect, and
making them approve a plan before they may speak is the gate running the
conversation.

**The planning call is still not remembered.** It was the one turn that is not,
and under this it is a compaction — so remembering it would put the draft in the
window twice, once as turns and once as the summary of those turns. The plan it
produces is the thing that survives, exactly as the sentence above says.

## Appended 2026-09-20, fourth section: built, and the four things the argument had wrong

§first argued it, §second built the wrong trigger, §third replaced it. This is
what the code says now that it exists, and it is `completed` because the thing
this file existed to produce has been produced.

**What landed.** `Job::plan` is `Option<Plan>` and `Turn::job` is `JobId`;
`JobState` is `Open` and `Closed`; the current job is derived from the jobs and
opened by a turn landing in it; `Context::rounds` holds every plan put up and
what answered it; `protocol::VERSION` is **7** and `record::FORMAT` is **16**,
with `job_proposed` and `job_rejected` kept parseable and never written again.
The gate's messages are `draft_opened`, `plan_proposed`, `plan_declined`,
`approve_plan`, `decline_plan` and `request_plan`.

**The invariant held.** No narrowing is built from a draft, `Plan::narrow` is
called at an approval and nowhere else, and the only change to what the sandbox
allows is none. What moved is what *reaches* it, which §third records and this
section does not soften.

### 1. The signature needed the id back, and it means something else now

§The proposal's *an id is what approval hands out* is right about the model and
was wrong about the wire, by one field. `crate::approval::Approval` binds a
signature to *a grant, in a session, for a job* — and a signature bound to no job
can be replayed against any of them. So `approve_plan` carries `job` after all,
`Option` and documented as **the id this approval is about to hand out, not one
that exists**. A client reads it as *one past the last job*; the server refuses a
mismatch rather than following it; an unsigned approval omits it.

That is not the old field renamed. The old one was an address and a lookup was
done with it; this one is never looked up, because the thing being approved is
not a job yet. It is the difference between *which job is this about* and *which
job will this create*, and the second is the only question a proposal can answer.

### 2. `DraftOpened` has to name its turn, or the first turn of every session
### reads as belonging to nothing

Found by a failing assertion and it is the item's own claim, inverted.
`turn_started` says which job a turn is *in*, and it goes out before the turn
runs — so the turn that **opens** a draft is in a job that did not exist when it
started, and its `TurnView::job` was `null`. A view folded from the wire would
have said *this turn belongs to no job* about the exact turn item 22 exists to
attribute.

So `draft_opened` carries `turn`, and the fold attributes it there. A draft opens
by receiving a turn; naming that turn is the message's own content rather than a
field added to it.

### 3. `Counts::Jobs` needed a shape field, and *absent* had to be an answer

§What it costs called this and it is worth saying how it ended: `Jobs` carries
`shape`, and `SessionView::shape()` reads it off the record format — `>= 16` is
`Alternation`, anything else is `Gated`. **Including absent**, which is the part
that was not obvious: the `format` field arrived with this change, so a store row
that has none was written by a binary from before it. Reading that as the current
shape would be the one mistake the field exists to prevent.

It also forced `SessionView::new` to stamp `record::FORMAT`. A view folded from
live events never meets a header, so without it the live fold and the replay of
the recording it is writing disagreed about the one field that says how to read
the rest — which is what `folding_live_events_gives_the_same_view_as_folding_the_record`
is for, and it caught it immediately.

### 4. A resume of an older stream gets one reconstructed draft, and it says so

§What it costs said the conversion is where the claim is made *or refused*, and
what it does is neither quite: a stream with jobless turns gets **one** draft,
inserted before every job the view carries, whose objective is
`RESTORED_DRAFT` — *turns resumed from a recording written before every turn had
a job*. Open, because nothing ever folded them and a resume must not invent a
summary that was never written.

The cost is stated where the code is: it is an open job that is not
`current_job`, which is the one place the alternation's *at most one job is open*
does not hold — and it holds for every stream written under it.

## Still open, after building it

- **The three from §first survive**: what the model is told when it is drafting,
  whether a draft's fold is worth its summary, and where a resume lands
  mid-draft. The last one is now answered for the *old* format by §4 above and
  unanswered for the new one, which is a smaller question than it was.
- **The draft's floor**, which §third promotes from a temptation to the next row:
  the session's policy narrowed to reads, so exploration cannot write.
- **What a 7B does with a planning call that has context.** The whole argument
  for `request_plan` reading the draft is that it is the first version of that
  call with more context than any other, and nothing here measured it. It is a
  row, and it is the first one that could plausibly move the gate's headline
  number.
- **`Round` has no turn number**, so *how many turns of drafting a plan took*
  cannot be answered from a recording, only *how many rounds*. Noticed while
  writing the counts and left, because the field is cheap and the question it
  answers has not been asked yet.
