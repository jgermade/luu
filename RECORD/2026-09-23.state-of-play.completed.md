## What the loss is, and what it is not

**It is** the first local measurement of the claim
[`luu-design.md`](../luu-design.md) §Goals calls *the key differentiator*, and
on this corpus no configuration of the window rules recovers any of the gap.
Rule A is inert, bit for bit, because the corpus attaches nothing a span rule
can act on. Rule C removes the evictions and the model pays for it by
re-reading. The managed arm's last turn is the best failure of the six — it
says accurately that every result came back pruned and it never saw the code —
and it is still a failure.

**It is not** a verdict on the rules in general, for reasons the record gives
itself. The corpus was built to make a model re-read one 2 000-line file, which
is the worst case for a rule that removes what was read. Five of six turns in
every 8K arm end at the 8-step tool limit, so part of the gap may be the limit
and not the window. And it is one seed and one corpus, on a server whose four
slots made nominally identical arms differ at turn 1.

**What it changes is the order, not the design — yet.** The new revision puts
the step limit first (row 36) because every other reading depends on it. It
keeps the one instrument that the result tempts building — a citation that
says where the bytes went, row 37 — behind that run. And it writes down the
decision the design now owes as a row of its own (41), with its preconditions
named. [`8k-against-96k-on-the-m4-pro`](2026-09-22.8k-against-96k-on-the-m4-pro.completed.md)
was right not to rewrite §Goals on one corpus. A row makes sure *not yet*
cannot turn into *never*.

**The rule A commitment held its first test.** The 2026-09-19 promise was that
a second corpus or model contradicting the run would flip the default back.
Both arrived: a 27B, and two corpora. On `long-session.txt` rule A does
nothing either way; on `edit-reread.txt` it fires and every re-asked value comes
back current. Neither contradicts, so the default stays, and it is still on
the evidence of small numbers.

## The late landing, and the filter that missed it

`dcab8f4` and `e3c501c` were authored at 01:13 and 01:22 on 2026-09-15 and

- **Item 6 was closed twice**, by
  [`the-panel-reads-pruned`](2026-09-15.the-panel-reads-pruned.completed.md) on
  the branch and
  [`the-panel-reads-the-pruned-lines`](2026-09-16.the-panel-reads-the-pruned-lines.completed.md)
  on `main`, a day apart. The branch's deferred bug — a reload that forgets
  eviction — was fixed by the `main` one before
  [`a-reload-remembers-eviction`](2026-09-15.a-reload-remembers-eviction.completed.md)
  landed to fix it. `store.js` reads `t.evicted_by ?? null` today, once. Two
  records for one row are not a problem for an append-only record. They are a
  measure of what a branch that waits six days costs: an afternoon, done twice.
- **Their *Still open* sections were never read by a cut.**

The mechanism is the filter.
[The last state of play](2026-09-21.state-of-play.completed.md) says
the revision was built from *the fourteen records written since 2026-09-17*,
and a record's filename carries the day it was written, not the day it landed.
This revision was harvested by landing instead:

```sh
git log --diff-filter=A --name-only --format= <last cut>^..HEAD -- 'RECORD/*.md'
```

The `^` is deliberate. The three records that closed rows 31–34 landed **in**
the last cut's commit, and their *Still open, after building it* sections were
written alongside it and harvested by nobody. Three of the new rows come from
them.
