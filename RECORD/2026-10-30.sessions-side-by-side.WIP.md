# Sessions side by side: several live sessions, each with its own container

**Status:** proposal; nothing of it is built. Roadmap row 14, *concurrency —
`serve` runs one live session at a time*, which has waited on a design
argument since [`state-of-play`](2026-09-09.state-of-play.completed.md).

> Los runners, serían los runtimes en otra máquina, elegibles desde la UI/CLI
>
> Cierto, entonces en realidad ya lo tenemos implementado con las sesiones
> remotas
>
> Podríamos habilitar tener varias sesiones activas, con varios contenedores en
> paralelo, uno para cada sesión

Three questions were put and answered: **a limit and an idle sleep**,
**engines shared, a second local model warned about**, **a folder per
session**.

## What there is today

One live session per `serve`. Choosing another one — new, or from the history
— checkpoints the live one, ends its worker and its container, and builds the
next in its place ([`multi-session-in-serve`](2026-09-04.multi-session-in-serve.completed.md)).
Switching is fast and nothing runs while you look elsewhere: a turn in one
session and a turn in another cannot happen at once.

`App` holds the live session's state beside the process's:

| The session's | The process's |
| --- | --- |
| `session`, `destination`, `agency` (its worker, its container), `posture`, `grants`, `tools_unlimited`, `view`, `stream`, `session_id`, `session_started_at`, `recorder`, `events` | `engines`, `store`, `approvers`, `tasks`, `icons`, `brands`, `postures`, `environments`, the flags |

**What `multi-session-in-serve` rejected, and why it no longer holds.** It
rejected *per-socket virtual sessions* "because `Agency` / `Sandbox` and worker
container processes are currently single-tenant per session lifecycle." Since
then every session builds its own `Agency` — sandbox, worker, container —
through `posture_for` and `finish`, and nothing in it is shared with another.
What is single-tenant now is `App`, and that is a struct, not a constraint.

## The proposal

### 1. `LiveSession`, and a map of them

```
App (the process)                    LiveSession (one per live session)
├─ engines, store, approvers         ├─ session, destination, agency → worker → container
├─ runtimes, tasks, icons, config    ├─ grants, view, stream, recorder, events
└─ live: Map<id, Arc<LiveSession>>   └─ folder, last_active
```

The session's fields move into `LiveSession` unchanged; `App` keeps the
process's and a map. A turn takes its session's locks, never another's, so two
turns run at once on two sessions and each holds its own gate.

### 2. Addressed, not switched

- `/ws?session=<id>` — the protocol, unchanged, carried for one session. A
  socket with no `session` is the most recently used one, which is what every
  client written before this connects to.
- `/api/session/*` → `/api/sessions/<id>/*`: the same routes, under the session
  they act on. The old paths answer for the most recently used one for a
  release, then go.
- `POST /api/sessions` makes a session **beside** the others. Resuming a stored
  one makes it live beside them too. Neither ends anything.
- `DELETE /api/sessions/<id>/live` ends one: checkpoint, end the worker.

### 3. A limit, and sleep

- `[sessions] max-live` (default 4). Making one more than that is refused,
  naming the live ones and the oldest idle one.
- `[sessions] idle-minutes` (default 30). A live session with no turn, no
  proposal waiting and no client for that long is **put to sleep**:
  checkpointed and its worker ended, exactly what a switch does today. It stays
  in the list; opening it resumes it, as the history does now. A session with a
  turn running or a gate open never sleeps.
- What `install` put in a sleeping session's container goes with the container
  ([`a-session-carries-its-policies`](2026-10-30.a-session-carries-its-policies.completed.md)
  §3); waking says so, as a move does.

### 4. Engines: shared, and a second model warned about

`engines::Supervisor` is already the process's. Sessions sending to the same
local model share its engine; it stops when no **live** session sends to it,
where today it stops when *the* session does not. Starting a session on a
second local model is allowed, and the dialog says what is already loaded and
how much memory it holds, before the click.

### 5. A folder per session

Each session has its folder, chosen under `serve`'s root and recorded in its
header; its sandbox base, its tree, its git panel, its repository map and its
selection are that folder's. This is roadmap row 49's *the folder chosen
becomes the session's base rather than a filter*, done for every session at
once. Two live sessions in one folder are allowed, and both are told — two
agents writing one tree is the person's call to make, not luu's to forbid.

### 6. The page

One session on screen at a time, as now. The sessions list marks each live
one: running, waiting at the gate, idle, asleep. Switching attaches the page
to another session and detaches from this one; this one keeps running. A
turn that ends or a gate that opens in a session not on screen is a badge on
its row.

## What it costs

- **The largest change to `serve` so far.** Nearly every handler in
  `serve.rs` reads the live session. Done in steps, each leaving luu working:
  1. `LiveSession` split out, with the map holding exactly one — no change in
     behaviour, every test as it is.
  2. The map, the addressed routes and sockets, `max-live` and sleep.
  3. A folder per session (row 49).
  4. The page: the list, attach and detach, badges.
  5. The engines' warning.
- **Memory and containers grow with live sessions**, which is what the limit
  and sleep are for.
- **The recordings do not change**: a session's stream was always its own.

## Rejected

- **A process per session** behind a router. Each `serve` would load its own
  engines, so two sessions on one model would hold it twice — the opposite of
  §4.
- **Runners on another machine** as a separate feature. A session on another
  host already runs entirely there (`[host.*]`), and side by side it can stay
  live while a local one runs.

## Still open

- Whether one person's two sessions in one folder should share a lock on
  writes, or only be told.
- A client of `/ws` that is not the page (an editor, some day): it addresses a
  session like the page does.

## Appended 2026-10-30: step 1, built

`LiveSession` holds the twelve fields that were the live session's —
`destination`, `session`, `events`, `recorder`, `agency`, `posture`, `grants`,
`tools_unlimited`, `view`, `session_started_at`, `session_id`, `stream` — with
their doc comments, moved as they were. `App` keeps the process's and one
`live: LiveSession`, a plain field rather than the map, so that every access
became `app.live.<field>` with no new lifetimes to reason about: a guard taken
from a field of a temporary `Arc` would not have outlived its statement, and
step 2 is where handlers will take their session as a local.

Done by a script, not by hand: the fields and their comments moved by name,
both constructors (`App::create` and the tests' `App` literal) had their
initialisers moved into `live: LiveSession { … }`, and every `app.<field>`,
`state.app.<field>` and — inside `impl App` only — `self.<field>` was
rewritten, method calls skipped (`app.agency()` and `app.destination()` are
`App`'s methods, and stay). The compiler found the rest: four accesses split
across lines, and `map_rendered` moved into `App` before `live`'s initialiser
borrowed it, which a reorder fixed.

**Verified as no change**: `cargo test --workspace`, clippy, fmt; every live
smoke spec, 75 passing, `terminal.spec`'s contained test skipping without a
`luu-worker:dev` image, and `workspace.spec`'s *diff in a modal* failing as it
did before — at the host shell, with and without this.

## Appended 2026-10-30: step 2, built

**The map.** `App.lives` holds `Arc<LiveSession>`s by id and which one is
current; `App::current`, `App::live(id)`, `App::all_live` and
`App::make_current` (which tells a `watch` channel). `destination()` and
`agency()` moved onto `LiveSession`; `App::publish` and `App::checkpoint` take
the session. Every `app.live.<field>` became `live.<field>`, and the compiler
said where `live` had to come from: handlers resolve it, the turn path —
`handle_client_message`, `on_prompt`, `request_plan`, `offer`,
`approve_plan`, `decline_plan`, `close_job`, `reopen_job`, `begin_turn`,
`start_turn`, `name_session`, `learn_window`, `refuse` — takes it as a
parameter, and `Watching` (a turn's ask and extend) carries it. A turn holds
only its own session's locks.

**New and resumed sessions are built beside, not in place.**
`LiveSession::blank` is a session with nothing in it; `create_session` and
`resume_session` run the code they always ran against it — every field is set
there anyway — and make it current at the end. Neither refuses because a turn
is running any more: that turn is another session's. A resume of a session
that is already live attaches to it as it is (and ignores a body naming
another destination — *Continue here* moves one). `follow_the_default`, which
made a new session to replace an untouched one, now puts the one it replaced
to sleep.

**Addressing differs from §2.** Not `/api/sessions/<id>/*` — every
session-scoped route written twice — but **`?session=<id>` on any route**, the
parameter `/ws` takes. `address_session`, a middleware inside the token
check, resolves it into a `tokio::task_local!` for the request; `App::addressed`
reads it, else the current one; a session that is not live is a 404; a host's
`/h/<name>/…` passes through. The trace socket resolves its session before the
upgrade, since the socket outlives the request. A protocol socket with no
`session` **follows the current one**: when `make_current` says another, it
re-subscribes and sends that session's `hello`, which is what the one socket
the page opens was always sent after a new session or a resume. A socket
counts as a client of its session (`Attached`) for as long as it is open.

**`max-live` sleeps before it refuses.** §3 said one more is refused. Until
step 4 the page has no way to close a session, so a person would meet that
refusal after four clicks on *New*. Instead the least recently used session
that may sleep — not current, no client, no turn, nothing at its gate, nothing
held — is put to sleep to make room, and the refusal is left for when none
may. `[sessions] max-live` and `idle-minutes` are in `provider::Sessions`;
`sleep_the_idle` runs once a minute from `bind`.

**The rest.** `release_engines` keeps every engine a live session sends to
(`stop_others` takes a list). `recorder` is an `Arc`, shared by the sessions
of one process, so `--record` keeps receiving every session's header as it
did. `list_sessions` puts the current session first as `live`, as the page
reads it, and every other live one under its own id from memory rather than
from the store. `view_for`, `rename_session` and `delete_session_handler` know
every live session by id: a live one cannot be deleted, and archiving one that
is not current puts it to sleep first.

**Tests that changed, and why.** Four `serve_ws` tests assumed a new session
or a resume ends the previous one: `multi_session_lifecycle_and_switching`
(deleting the session it switched away from), `a_resume_keeps_the_posture_…`
(the refusal is about a session rebuilt elsewhere), `a_session_can_be_named_…`
(renaming one that is still live) and `a_recording_that_spans_sessions_…` (a
resume that re-bases the file). Each now puts that session to sleep first, or
expects it live, and says so where it does. New:
`two_sessions_live_side_by_side` — a turn in a session that is not current,
its own socket, `?session=` over HTTP, 404s for one not live, sleep and resume;
`one_session_too_many_puts_the_oldest_idle_one_to_sleep`.

**Verified**: `cargo test --workspace`, clippy, fmt; every live smoke spec, 76
of 77, `workspace.spec`'s *diff in a modal* failing as before. The page is
unchanged, and its *New session* now leaves the previous one live behind it.

## Appended 2026-10-30: step 3, built

**A session's folder is its sandbox's base.** The agency factory takes a
folder (`AgencyFactory` gained an `Option<PathBuf>`), `SandboxArgs` carries it
as a non-flag field, and `resolve_from` uses it in place of `current_dir()` —
so a policy file's relative paths resolve against the session's folder, which
is what they mean. `folder_under_root` is the one check: a directory, under
the root `serve` was started in, relative from the root or absolute under it,
`None` for the root itself.

**Where it comes from.** `POST /api/sessions {folder}`. A resume makes the
session again in the folder its posture recorded — `record::Posture.folder`,
filled by `Agency::posture` from the sandbox's base, so headers and views carry
it with no plumbing (`record::FORMAT` 27, the page's with it); `same_place`
compares it where both runs said; a recorded folder that is no longer one
under the root resumes at the root, said on stderr. A runtime move and a
posture move keep the folder. `follow_the_default` makes its replacement in
the same folder.

**The map and the walk are the session's.** `LiveSession` holds
`map_rendered` and `walked`; `App::map_for` builds both over an agency's
sandbox under the budget and order `serve` was started with, on the blocking
pool, and `create_session` and `resume_session` build them for the new
session's folder — the resume's context is rebuilt with that map.

**`PUT /api/session/folder`** moves a session before its first turn: its
agency made again over the new folder (its copy of the lists, else its posture
by name, else the server's own file), its map and walk rebuilt, its context
reset with the new map, and `settle_move`'s header written. After the first
turn it is a 409: the history is about the tree it worked in. Where it already
is answers `moved: false` first, whatever it has done — found by the smoke
specs, where a page's *Use this folder* on a server whose session already had
turns was refused for asking to stay put.

**The page.** The panels' base is the session's folder, and the old *root*
filter is always `""`. `GET /api/folders` lists directories under the root —
the picker's listing, which went through the session's sandbox and so could
not climb above its folder; a host on an older `luu` falls back to its
workspace tree. *Use this folder* remembers the choice by the root, moves a
fresh session there, and starts the panels over; a session with turns stays,
and the picker stays open saying so and that new sessions will start there. On
a visit, a fresh session is moved into the remembered folder, which is what the
remembered root always did. When another session comes on screen its
`settings.base` differs, and the panels follow (`followFolder`). The new
session's dialog sends `folder` (the one remembered, else the one on screen's).
`/api/settings` adds `root`, `folder` and `shared_with`.

**Git from a subfolder.** Porcelain v1 says paths from the repository's top
wherever git runs, so a session in a subfolder saw paths that matched nothing
in its tree. `git_status` strips `git rev-parse --show-prefix` and leaves out
what is outside the folder. This was latent before: `serve` started below a
repository's top had the same mismatch.

**Verified**: `serve_ws::a_session_works_in_its_own_folder` — a session in
`sub/` lists `sub/`'s files, the picker's listing is the root's, `..` is a 400,
a second session in the folder is told it shares it, a fresh session moves to
the root and one with a turn is a 409, and asleep and resumed it is back in
`sub`. `workspace::tests::status_from_a_subfolder_is_said_from_the_subfolder`.
`cargo test --workspace`, clippy, fmt. Every live smoke spec: 74 passing;
`workspace.spec`'s *diff in a modal* failing as before; and `theme.spec`'s
*the index marks the section being read* failing in two full runs and passing
three times alone — a static demo page no part of this reaches, so read as
load, not as this change. `gate.spec`'s *narrowed to a subdirectory* passes as
written; its comment now says the folder is the session's.
