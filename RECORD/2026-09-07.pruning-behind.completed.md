# Pruning behind: the history was never going to get smaller

**Status:** rule B of
[`what-leaves-the-history`](2026-09-06.what-leaves-the-history.WIP.md), built,
measured, and off by default. With
[`a-span-is-rendered-once`](2026-09-07.a-span-is-rendered-once.completed.md)
that record is now spent, and this one closes it.

The proposal's central claim about B was wrong, and the run says so in one row.

## What B was expected to be, and what it is

> B is where the 91% lives, and it is the same trade [`Eviction::Block`] already
> made and measured: a deep, infrequent rewrite beats a shallow one every turn.
> — [`what-leaves-the-history`](2026-09-06.what-leaves-the-history.WIP.md) §2

"The 91%" is the share of the history block that is quoted code by turn 20. The
step the proposal took from that number to *B is the token win* is the part that
does not hold: **the history block is 91% code because code is what fits, not
because code is what is extra.** Take it out and the block does not shrink — the
turns eviction had been dropping move back in, and they are not free.

Four arms, twenty grounded turns, `--select-tokens 1024 --context-limit 8192`,
in [`runs/2026-09-07.pruning-behind/`](runs/2026-09-07.pruning-behind/):

| | `off` | A | **B** | both |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| prompt tokens | 125 408 | 119 607 | **126 943** | 121 671 |
| cuts | 12 | 8 | **0** | **0** |
| turns evicted | **13** | **13** | **0** | **0** |
| turns that stopped quoting | 0 | 0 | 15 | 15 |
| mean prefix reuse | 34.0% | 52.1% | 38.9% | **54.5%** |

**B costs 1.2% more tokens and evicts nothing.** Turns 1–13 leave the window in
both arms that do not prune and stay in both that do. So B is not a token
mechanism at all — it is a **fidelity** mechanism, and it was ordered, argued
and named as the other thing.

The clean way to see it: the last turn of `off` carries **6 124** tokens of
history and the last turn of `b` carries **6 121** — the same block to within
three tokens, holding **7 turns** in the first and **20** in the second. That is
the proposal's own opening sentence arriving as a measurement instead of an
observation.

## The proposal's mechanism, and the three places building it moved

### 1. It is a rung above eviction, not a policy beside it

The proposal described B as a rule about rendering and left *when it fires*
open. Making it fire on its own schedule would need a second low-water knob and
a second argument for deep-and-infrequent. It does not need either: **pruning
runs when the window no longer fits, before eviction, against the same target
`--evict` computes.** A pruned turn keeps its question and its answer and loses
the bytes it was answered from, which a path can fetch back; an evicted turn
loses the conversation, which nothing can. So the recoverable half is spent
first and eviction only reaches what pruning could not save.

That is also why the numbers came out the way they did. B is not competing with
eviction for tokens, it is *displacing* it.

### 2. Under rule A, pruning a turn can make the window bigger

The two rules interact in a way neither record predicted, and a test now says
it. Under [`Repeat::Once`] a shared span is charged to the window **once**,
whoever renders it. Pruning its owner therefore does not remove it: the charge
moves to the next carrier and a tombstone is added on top, and the window grows.
Pruning pays for a shared span only once the watermark has passed its *youngest*
carrier.

The loop is a linear scan from the current watermark that stops at the first
level that fits, so this costs nothing in correctness — it terminates, and it
never prunes a turn a smaller watermark would have spared. It does mean *B's
saving is not monotone in the watermark*, which is the kind of thing that reads
as a bug the first time a fixture hits it.

`item_cost` reads rule B before rule A for the same reason ownership is decided
inside the window: **a pruned turn is showing nothing, so it can neither take a
span over nor give one up.** A span two turns carry whose older one has been
pruned is rendered by the younger — the orphan rule again, one mechanism along.

### 3. The tombstone lost its third field

The proposal wanted `[read path:453-464 — 312 tokens, dropped at turn 31]`. It
ships as the path, the span and the size, without the turn number: the watermark
is one number and the turns below it were pruned at different times, so labelling
every tombstone with the latest move would be false and keeping a per-turn record
of it is a store for something the trace already carries better. `pruned` is a
protocol message — the turns that stopped quoting, what it freed, and the
counter — which is exactly what `evicted` is and for the same argued reason.

A model cannot act on "turn 31" anyway. It can act on the path, and `read_file`
is in the prompt already.

## What it costs

- **A protocol bump and a format bump**, 5 → 6 and 7 → 8, reusing the numbers
  the un-made `imported` bump briefly took. `ServerMessage::Pruned` is on the
  protocol channel rather than the trace channel for `Evicted`'s reason pushed
  one step further: a pruned turn is *still in the transcript*, so a client that
  could not parse this would draw a grounded-looking turn whose grounding is not
  in the prompt — a worse lie than the one that made eviction a message. The
  debug client marks it, differently from an evicted turn, because they are
  different losses.
- **Four tests that had hard-coded the wire version broke.** They now read
  `VERSION` and `FORMAT`; a version bump should break the tests that are about
  versions and no others.
- **+1.2% prompt tokens on this corpus**, which is the honest headline and not a
  rounding error to bury.
- **The watermark is not persisted.** A resumed session starts it at the floor.
  Fragments are moot — the store never kept them, so a resumed turn quotes
  nothing either way — but tool results come back, and a resumed session prunes
  them again from scratch rather than remembering that it had.
- **Nothing here knows whether a 7B can use a tombstone.** See below; it is the
  whole open question and this run cannot touch it.

## What was rejected

- **A low-water knob of pruning's own.** Two knobs, two arguments, and the
  second one already made. `--evict`'s target does it.
- **Pruning instead of evicting rather than before it.** A window can be too
  small for the conversation alone; eviction has to stay reachable, and it is —
  it runs on whatever pruning could not free.
- **Summarising a pruned block with the model.** Prose in the write-once region,
  rejected in the proposal and rejected again here. The deterministic line is the
  whole of the middle ground.
- **Un-pruning when the window opens up.** The watermark is monotone for the
  reason [`Context::floor`] is: a history whose front moves is the one thing a
  prefix cache cannot survive. There is a test that opens the window right back
  up and asserts nothing comes back.
- **Pruning `Turn::code_context` itself.** It is a render rule, like A. The
  bytes stay, which is what lets the fold still quote them verbatim at close —
  the proposal left that as an open thread and the answer is *nothing to do*,
  with a test rather than a sentence.

## Still open

- **Does a 7B answer from a tombstone?** The protocol is
  [`can-a-model-read-a-tombstone.WIP`](2026-09-07.can-a-model-read-a-tombstone.WIP.md).
  It is not run here and cannot be: this machine has no GPU, four cores, and the
  network policy refuses `ollama.com`. Until it is run, B is a fidelity claim
  with a measured cost and an unmeasured benefit, which is why it is off.
- **Whether tool results and fragments want the same rule.** They ship under one
  flag and the proposal flagged this as unargued. It still is. The asymmetry the
  fold already encodes — `steps` are evidence a job produced, `shown` is context
  it was handed — is the place a second look would start.
- **Whether 15 turns is too many to prune at once.** The watermark takes the
  eviction target, which under `--evict turn` means "as far as it takes". A
  gentler rung — prune the oldest *n* — was not tried because there is no
  measurement that would tell the two apart without a model.
- **The +1.2%.** On a corpus with longer answers the conversation B saves is
  worth more and the trade moves. Nobody has run that corpus.
