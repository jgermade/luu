# The newest body wins

**Status:** completed — it plans code, and the code is in the same commit.

It takes the decision
[`one-path-two-bodies`](2026-09-19.one-path-two-bodies.completed.md) argued and
deliberately did not take, and it takes it on the number that record said it was
waiting for. Item 19 of [`ROADMAP/2026-09-17`](../ROADMAP/2026-09-17/README.md).

## What it was waiting for, and what arrived

The defect is one prompt carrying one path under two bodies, because
`code_context` is written once when a turn closes and every turn re-reads its
own spans. The detector landed on the 19th; the repair did not, in one sentence:

> The frequency the trade turns on has **no** number, and cannot have one until
> the detector has run against a corpus that edits files.

Three things landed between that sentence and this file, and all three were
built for other questions:

- [`a-corpus-that-edits`](2026-09-19.a-corpus-that-edits.completed.md) — thirteen
  turns, two edits, and **8 of 13 renders** carrying two bodies for one path.
- [`one-counting-surface`](2026-09-19.one-counting-surface.completed.md) — the
  tally that made *8 of 13* a number a command prints rather than a person's
  count, and beside it `repeated` = 7 against `diverged` = 8 in one session.
- `prefix_reuse`, on the wire since long before any of this, which is the other
  half of the trade and which nobody had thought to read for this question.

So the bar the record set itself is met, and the decision is: **under
`Repeat::Once` the newest body of a path wins, and the older ones go out as the
line that cites them.**

## The cost, which is the part the argument had wrong

Measured the way this project measures a rule: the same corpus, the same flags,
and one thing different — here the binary, because the repair is not a flag.
`before` is `2e87d2c` and `after` is this commit.
[`runs/2026-09-20.the-newest-body-wins/`](runs/2026-09-20.the-newest-body-wins/).

| turn | reuse before | reuse after |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2–5 | 79.7 / 86.7 / 91.6 / 97.3% | **unchanged** |
| **6** | 90.5% | **47.7%** |
| 7 | 97.9% | 97.8% |
| 8 | 97.8% | 97.5% |
| 9 | 98.3% | 98.2% |
| 10 | 88.7% | 88.1% |
| 11 | 98.2% | 98.2% |
| **12** | 93.6% | **63.1%** |
| 13 | 98.2% | 98.0% |

Session prefix reuse **93.95% → 87.14%**, and the table is the whole finding:
**every point of it is in turns 6 and 12**, which are the two turns that re-read
the file the corpus edits. Ten of the twelve are inside 0.3 points of where they
were.

**The break is one per edit and not one per render**, and that is what the
argument on the 19th did not see. It feared a rule that rewrites the history
block on every call. What rewrites it is the render that *discovers* the edit;
after that the citation standing in the old block's place is itself stable, so
turns 7 to 11 reuse exactly what they reused before. That is the ratchet, below,
seen from the prefix's side.

**And the window got smaller, which nothing predicted.** A citation is cheaper
than the bytes it replaces, so the history bucket over the session fell **7 200 →
6 432 tokens** and every prompt after the first edit is 77 to 154 tokens lighter.
The repair is not paid for out of the window; it pays the window back. Rule A's
own +18 points of prefix reuse are spent to the extent of 6.81 of them, twice,
on a corpus whose edits are two in thirteen turns — and what is bought is that
**8 of 13 prompts stop contradicting themselves**.

Stated as the trade it is: *at the turn a file changes, the model re-reads a
prompt it has mostly seen before; on every turn after, it reads one that is both
smaller and true.*

## What is built

**The rule.** `Current` names, per path, the body carried by the newest turn that
read it, with the turn being asked ahead of all of them. A fragment whose bytes
are not that body renders as `// path — N tokens, superseded`. Everything else
is untouched.

**Derived, not stored, and that is the ratchet.** §The fix asked for one and
expected a third instance of the floor-and-prune-line shape: a field that only
moves forward. It is not a field. `Current` is computed over every turn the
session holds — **the evicted ones included** — so the newest reader of a path
only ever moves forward, because turns are only ever appended. A body the session
watched go stale cannot come back by anything *leaving* the window. That is
`SessionView::counts()`'s argument from the day before, one structure along: a
fact derived from what the session already holds cannot drift from it, and a
stored ratchet is a second implementation of the finding sitting beside it.

The case that decides the shape is a file **edited back to what it was**. A
ratchet over *bodies ever found stale* would keep citing turn 1's bytes after
turn 3 re-read them and found them there — citing the only body in the window
that was true. A ratchet over *readers* does not, because turn 3 is a later
reader and its body is current by construction. Pinned as
`the_edit_and_the_edit_back_are_both_superseded_by_the_newest_read`.

**Unguarded, which is where it parts company with rule B.** A pruned span's
citation is not taken where it costs more than the span, on the rule that a
citation which is not cheaper is not a saving. This one is taken always: what it
replaces is not expensive, it is **untrue**, and a two-line fragment being cheap
to send is not a reason to send bytes the session knows are stale. The window
pays a few tokens for it on the small spans and the `history` bucket says so,
because that bucket describes the messages rather than estimating them.

**Rule A's, and not a fourth window rule.** `Current` is empty under
`Repeat::Always`, so a run made without rule A is byte-identical to what it was.
That is the same property every one of rule A's costs is built to keep, and it is
what leaves the two arms one flag apart — the 2026-09-19 numbers are still
producible, under `--no-repeat-once`, which is what the probe now asserts.

**The way back is the flag that already exists.** No `--no-supersede`. Under
`Repeat::Always` two bodies for one path is documented behaviour: every turn
re-sends what it selected and the blocks read as per-turn snapshots. Under
`Repeat::Once` the rule's promise is in its name, and shipping a known-false
prompt behind a flag would be offering *a span is rendered once* with an
asterisk. The enum stays at two values and the arms stay comparable.

**It reports, because the default has to.** A `superseded` trace line,
`record::FORMAT` 14 → 15, `Supersession` in `Counts`, and a row in `luu count`.
That is `one-counting-surface`'s rule applied to the thing it was written about:
the number is computed in the render and subtracted from a bucket, and a
difference is not a measurement of what made it.

## What the format number now has to carry

This is the one bump here that makes an **older line mean something new**.

A stream at 12, 13 or 14 carries `diverged` lines because the defect was reported
and not repaired. A stream at 15 carries `superseded` where those would have
been. So `diverged` empty at 15 is *nothing went stale* **or** *everything stale
was replaced*, and the two are told apart by the line beside it rather than by
the absence of one. A reader who counts divergences across the corpus without
reading the format number will find them falling to zero on 2026-09-20 and
conclude the sessions stopped editing files.

`Supersession`'s doc comment says this where a reader of the tally will meet it,
and `record::FORMAT`'s says it where a reader of the stream will.

## What is rejected

**A flag.** Above.

**Re-reading every fragment at render time**, which `one-path-two-bodies`
rejected for the right reason and which this record does not reopen:
`Context::select` holds no sandbox, and what a now-denied or deleted file does to
an old turn's render is item 20's argument. What landed there — a resume reads
its spans again — remains the one place the defect is *repaired* rather than
worked around, and it stays the one place.

**Widening the dedup to path-only**, also rejected on the 19th and worth one line
here because the measurement makes the case stronger than the argument did: the
oldest body would have won, the prompt would have been consistent and wrong, and
the history bucket would have fallen by roughly the same amount. A number that
looks like this one can be bought by being wrong, which is why *which* body wins
was the decision and not *how many*.

**Counting bodies once the fix is in.** `Diverged` stays exactly as it is, still
computed over what was sent, still reporting under `Repeat::Always`. Deleting it
would make the repair unfalsifiable: the only evidence that the fix works is that
the detector, unchanged, finds nothing.

## Found while doing it

**The fold had to be told which rule the last render ran.** `Context::cited`
exists because a fold has to cost its turns the way the prompt it is leaving
actually carried them, and that turned out to be exactly one field short: under
`Repeat::Once` a stale span leaves as a citation, and a fold that charged it at
its bytes would report a saving the window never had. `resending: Repeat` is
beside `cited`, set on every selection for the same stated reason — *it describes
the render and not the decision*. Second time in three days that a new field's
justification was already written down beside an older one.

**Four decision sites and one render site had to agree, and the file already knew
it.** `fits_from`, `window_tokens`, `prune_to` and `item_tokens` all learned
about supersession, because `buckets` describes `messages` rather than estimating
it and a floor chosen against bytes that will not be sent is a floor chosen
against the wrong window. The alternative — let the decision see the old cost and
document the slack, which is what `code_tokens` does for the turn being asked —
does not survive here: that approximation is safe because the direction is
one-way, and an **unguarded** citation can be dearer than the span it replaces,
so this one has no safe direction.

**`--select-tokens` doubled the lines and not the renders, again.** Sixteen lines
over the same eight renders, on `src/greeting.rs:8-10` — `greet` itself, a span
nobody typed. It is the same shape `one-counting-surface` found for `repeated`
the day before and for the same reason: most of what rule A touches is a fact
about the *selector*, which re-scores the same prompt-relevant file every turn.
The repair inherits that exactly, which is the right answer and is now asserted
rather than assumed.

## Still open

- **Whether a model notices either the defect or the repair.** Unchanged from
  `one-path-two-bodies` §Still open and not weakened by any of the above: the
  edits here are scripted, so 8 of 13 is a fact about the corpus. It is the same
  bar item 5 is held to, and this record does not get to skip it because its
  number is large. **This is Section A work** — a run against a model — and it is
  the only thing between the repair and a claim about sessions rather than about
  this corpus.
- **The line range is still part of the path.** Two overlapping specs of one file
  contradict each other invisibly to the detector, and the fix inherits the blind
  spot exactly, because it reads the same identity. `src/tally.rs` in the corpus
  is that case, grounded `1-6` and then `1-16`, and it stays silent in both arms.
- **The prefix cost is measured on a corpus with two edits in thirteen turns.**
  Whether the shape holds — all of the loss at the edits, none between them — is
  a property of the ratchet and ought to, but a session that edits every turn
  would pay at every turn, and nothing here has run one.
- **Nothing says which body won.** The citation says a span was superseded and
  not where the current bytes are, on the reasoning that they may not be in the
  window at all once the fresh reader is evicted. Whether a model does better
  with a pointer than with a bare fact is the same question `pruned_fragment_text`
  has never had answered.
