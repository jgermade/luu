# 8K against 96K on the M4 Pro, one flag apart

**Status:** completed — it is a run and its write-up. It runs §5 of
[`the-16gb-threshold`](2026-09-16.the-16gb-threshold.WIP.md) on **machine 3** rather
than machine 4, so it answers the *shape* of roadmap item 2 (window as `luu`'s own
flag, not a machine) and **not** the RTX half of it: the 27B fit test on the 16 GB
card is still owed and nothing here says anything about it.

Evidence: [`runs/2026-09-22.8k-against-96k-m4-pro/`](runs/2026-09-22.8k-against-96k-m4-pro/).

## What was run

- **Machine:** MacBook M4 Pro, 48 GB unified. Metal, `-ngl 99`.
- **Model:** `ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-IQ3_S.gguf`, 11 771 546 784 bytes — the
  article's own build, not the `qwen3.8:27b-mlx` (nvfp4) tag that was already in
  Ollama. That one is a different packing and would have been a different instrument.
- **Tokenizer:** `tokenizer.json` from `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B`, since the GGUF repo has none.
- **Server:** the `llama-server` bundled in `Ollama.app` (`0.1.2-dev`, commit
  `9d77fa172`), started **once**:
  `-c 98304 --cache-type-k q8_0 --cache-type-v q8_0 -fa on --jinja --temp 1.0
  --top-p 0.95 --top-k 20 --min-p 0.00 --port 8100`. The log says
  `n_ctx_slot = 98304`, so the server's window was 96K for both arms.
- **The one flag:** `luu chat --script … --context-limit {8192,98304} --reserve 512`.
  Nothing else differs between arms.

## First corpus, and why it says nothing

`grounded.txt`, 20 prompts, both arms. Both arms answered the same questions with the
same content, and **neither evicted anything**: the largest prompt in the 8192 arm was
3 106 tokens against a budget of 8 192. §5 said 96K would not evict on a 20-prompt
script; it did not say 8K would not either, and that is the part it got wrong. The two
arms differ by sampling only (temperature 1.0, unseeded, n=1 each), so this is a tie
that could not have been anything else. Not evidence for or against the differentiator.

Observed in passing: 1 empty reply in the 8192 arm and 3 in the 98304 arm, 20–75
completion tokens each and no visible text. Not chased; probably reasoning consuming
the turn, which is a guess.

## Second corpus: `long-session.txt`

Six prompts, nothing attached, so the model has to go and read. Run with
`--temperature 0 --seed 1` in both arms. Here the 8K arm does evict.

| | 8192 | 98304 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `evicted` events | 3 (turns 1–2, 3–4, 5) | 0 |
| turns that ended in an answer (`[stop]`) | **1 of 6** | **4 of 6** |
| turns that hit the 8-step tool limit | 5 | 2 |
| tool calls in the session | 40 | 21 |
| `read_file` calls, and on `context.rs` | 20, 19 | 12, 11 |
| distinct `read_file` argument sets | 16 | 12 |

Turns 1 and 2 are identical in both arms (the model exploring, capped at the tool
limit either way). The arms part at turn 3, *how does the stable prefix survive a new
turn?*: at 8K turns 1–2 have been evicted (15 358 tokens) and the model starts over
from `list_dir`, then re-reads `context.rs`, and never answers. At 96K it still has what
it read, reads four more ranges and answers with a five-point account of the
prefix ratchet that matches the file.

The last turn is the clearest one. *"summarise everything above in three lines"*:

- **8192:** three lines about the *system prompt* — "I'm luu, a concise local coding
  agent… I have file tools…". By turn 6 the history had been evicted down to
  nothing, and the model summarised the only thing left. Not a wrong summary of the
  conversation; a summary of a different text.
- **98304:** three lines that cover the budget's buckets, the prefix ratchet and the
  eviction order, which is what the session was about.

## Reading it

Of the three outcomes §5 named — 96K answers what 8K gets wrong, a tie, 8K wins — it is
the first, **on this corpus, one seed, n=1 per arm**. The outcome was not written down
before looking, which the protocol asked for; the first corpus had already come back a
tie by the time the second was chosen, and that ordering is on the record here rather
than tidied away.

What it is worth is narrower than the headline:

- It is the **rival thesis's** case, and the strongest form of it: an agent that has
  to *explore* and whose reads are large. The differentiator is not exercised, because
  both arms ran with the defaults — rule A on, B and C off, no map, no selection. The
  8K arm is "eviction only", not "context management at its best", so this says that
  *8K with today's defaults loses to 96K on exploratory work*, not that *a well-chosen
  8K loses to a badly-chosen 96K*.
- The mechanism is visible and it is the one the project already names: an evicted turn
  takes its tool output with it, and the model pays to read it again. 19 reads of one
  file in 6 turns is that cost, counted.
- One seed at temperature 0 is one path. The 98304 arm's two tool-limit turns and the
  8192 arm's five could each move by a run.

## What it cost, and what is not comparable

- **Timings mean nothing.** Swap reached 12.4 of 13.3 GB during the first corpus and one
  turn stalled for about six minutes at 0.03 t/s. The machine was carrying other work.
- **Not comparable with the article's RTX figures** or with any run on another box:
  silicon, serving stack and build of `llama-server` all differ. `machines.md`'s rule
  applies unchanged.
- **The download took several restarts** (a stalled `curl`, resumed with `-C -`); the
  file size matches the repository's, and no checksum was compared.

## Still open

- **Whether the differentiator closes the gap.** The run that matters is the same
  `long-session.txt` at 8192 with `--repeat-once --prune-behind` (and `--prune-results`),
  against the 98304 arm above. The corpus and flags exist; this record only measured the
  baseline they have to beat.
- **A second seed, and a second corpus that overflows 8K.** Everything above is one path.
- **The RTX half.** The 27B fit test on 16 GB (§3) and the MTP profile are untouched.
- **The 27B's tool-call format.** It emits `<tool_call>{…}</tool_call>` and leaks a
  `</think>`, where the corpus expects a fenced ` ```tool ` block. `luu` executed the
  calls, so it is tolerated, but it belongs to
  [`a-grammar-for-tool-calls`](2026-09-06.a-grammar-for-tool-calls.completed.md) and
  not here.
- **Whether the 8-step tool limit is the thing being measured.** Five of six 8K turns
  end at it. A higher limit might let the 8K arm finish, at more evictions; nothing here
  varies it.
- **The design document is unchanged.** [`local-first`](2026-09-01.local-first.completed.md)
  owes the claim a demonstration; this is the first local number on the rival side of
  it, and it is not yet a reason to rewrite `luu-design.md`.

## Appended 2026-09-22: the third arm, and it does not close the gap

The first *Still open* bullet asked for `long-session.txt` at 8192 with
`--repeat-once --prune-behind --prune-results`, against the 96K arm. That is this arm,
same server, same `--temperature 0 --seed 1`, same corpus. Evidence:
`long-8192-managed.txt` and `long-8192-managed.pruned.txt` in the runs directory.

| | 8192 plain | 8192 managed | 98304 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `evicted` events | 3 | **0** | 0 |
| `pruned` events | 0 | 4 | 0 |
| turns that ended in an answer | 1 of 6 | **1 of 6** | 4 of 6 |
| tool calls in the session | 40 | **40** | 21 |
| `read_file` calls, and on `context.rs` | 20, 19 | **27, 26** | 12, 11 |
| distinct `read_file` argument sets | 16 | 21 | 12 |

**The rules did their job and the job did not help.** Pruning replaced eviction
outright: zero `evicted` events, four `pruned` ones (14 480, 14 227, 8 429 and 7 302
tokens), so the *exchange* of every turn stayed in the window while the bytes it had
read left it. Answers did not move — one of six, as in the plain arm — and the model
read `context.rs` **more** often, 26 times against 19, which is the cost of a result
becoming a citation counted in the same unit as before.

The last turn is the informative one, and it differs from both other arms:

- **plain 8192:** summarised the system prompt, the history having gone entirely.
- **managed 8192:** *"You asked five questions about the context manager… every
  `read_file` result came back pruned, so I never actually saw the code… there's no
  technical content to summarize yet."* The history it kept is accurate, and it says
  exactly what it lacks. That is a better failure than the plain arm's, and it is
  still a failure: the answer the 96K arm gives is not in this window.
- **98304:** three faithful lines.

**What this does and does not say.** It is a first observation on
[roadmap item 5](../ROADMAP/2026-09-21/README.md), *whether a model minds losing tool
output to a citation*: on this path, it does, and it pays by asking again. But all three
flags were on together, so **rule C cannot be told apart from the other two here**, and
this is one seed on one corpus in which the model already spends its 8 steps per turn
reading the same 2 000-line file. An agent that re-reads is the worst case for a rule
that removes what was read, and the corpus was chosen to make it read. Not evidence
that rules B and C are wrong; evidence that they do not rescue this session.

**A note on how the arm was run.** The first attempt failed at turn 1 with
`transport: error sending request`: the `llama-server` had exited ("cleaning up before
exit") after the previous arm, without being stopped by hand. It was restarted with the
same model and flags, detached from the shell, and the arm re-run from turn 1. The
server's prompt cache was cold for it, which affects timing and nothing counted above.

**Still open, added.**

- **Rule A alone, and rule B alone**, against this baseline: the three flags moved
  together, so this arm cannot say which of them did what.
- **Whether a citation that says *where* the bytes went** (the pointer that
  [`the-newest-body-wins`](2026-09-20.the-newest-body-wins.completed.md) §Still open
  asks about) would let the model re-read one range instead of ranging over the file.
- **The 8-step limit again.** 5 of 6 turns end at it in both 8K arms; a corpus whose
  questions are answerable in fewer reads would show whether the gap is about the
  window or about a model that explores until it is told to stop.

## Appended 2026-09-22, third section: `--prune-behind` alone confirms the prediction, and isolates rule C

The two arms the second section asked for: `--prune-behind` alone, and
`--prune-behind --prune-results` without `--repeat-once` — as close as the flags allow to
*rule C alone*, since `--prune-results` is inert without `--prune-behind` per `luu chat
--help`. Same server, corpus, `--temperature 0 --seed 1`. Evidence:
`long-8192-prune-behind.txt` and `long-8192-prune-behind-results.txt`.

| | plain | repeat-once | prune-behind | prune-behind+results | managed (all three) | 98304 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `evicted` | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `pruned` | 0 | 0 | **2, 0 tokens each** | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| answered | 1/6 | 1/6 | 1/6 | 1/6 | 1/6 | 4/6 |
| tool calls | 40 | 40 | 40 | 40 | 40 | 21 |
| `read_file` on `context.rs` | 19 | 19 | 19 | 27 | 26 | 11 |

**`--prune-behind` alone is exactly what §Still open predicted: it fires and finds
nothing.** Both `pruned` events carry `"turns":[],"tokens":0` — the rule ran, looked at
the turns behind the line, and had no span to take from them, because this corpus
attaches none. Eviction count, tokens evicted (15 372 vs 15 358, 18 826 vs 18 826, 8 915
vs 8 915) and the answered count are the plain arm's, inside run-to-run noise. The
prediction that rule A/rule-behind-alone can't touch a tool-result-only session is now a
measurement, not an inference from the code.

**Isolating rule C confirms the second section's attribution.** `--prune-behind
--prune-results` reproduces the managed arm's shape closely — 0 evicted, 4 pruned, 1 of 6
answered, and `read_file` on `context.rs` at 27 against the managed arm's 26 — with
`--repeat-once` removed from the flags. The remaining one-count difference is inside the
same noise the paragraph below names, not a sign that rule A was doing anything: the
second section already showed rule A firing zero times on this corpus, and this arm
without it lands in the same place.

**A caveat the earlier sections didn't have occasion to raise: turn 1 is not
bit-identical across all five 8192 arms**, despite `--temperature 0 --seed 1` in every
one. `repeat-once` reproduced the plain arm's transcript exactly; `prune-behind` and
`prune-behind-results` each diverge from it starting at turn 1 — a different `grep`
flag choice, tool-call ordering, and small prompt-token deltas (6636 vs 6642). The
server serves 4 slots and reuses prefixes by longest-common-prefix across whatever else
has run in a slot (`selected slot by LCP similarity` in its own log), so a temp-0 seed
pins the sampler and not the batch composition. **The qualitative pattern — which rule
fires, whether it prunes anything, how many turns get answered — held across five
separate arms regardless**, which is the more load-bearing kind of reproducibility for
what this file is measuring, but exact token counts should not be read as more precise
than they are.

**Still open, updated.** The two bullets above are closed. What is left: whether a
citation that names *where* the current bytes are changes anything, the 8-step limit,
and everything in §Still open under *What it cost* about this box not being comparable
to the RTX.

## Appended 2026-09-22, second section: rule A alone is inert on this corpus

The first appended section's *Still open* asked for rule A alone against the plain 8192
arm, because the managed arm moved three flags at once. This is that arm:
`--repeat-once` and nothing else. Evidence: `long-8192-repeat-once.txt` and
`run-item2d.sh` in the runs directory.

**It is the plain arm, exactly.** The prompt and completion figures on every one of the
six `[stop]`/`[tool limit reached]` lines are identical to `long-8192.txt`, and so is the
transcript with the tool lines stripped: 40 tool calls, 20 `read_file` (19 on
`context.rs`), 3 `evicted`, 1 of 6 answered. The trace carries **no `repeated` event at
all**, so the rule did not fire once.

The reason is the one roadmap item 28 already names, seen from the other side. Rule A
collapses a *span* — a fragment or a selected range — that several turns carry, and this
corpus attaches nothing: everything the model reads arrives as a `read_file` result, and
a result is not a span. Rule A has nothing here to act on. So the managed arm's four
`pruned` events, 14 480 to 7 302 tokens each, can only have come from `--prune-results`,
which is the flag that lets a turn behind the line give up a tool's *output* — rule C.
`--prune-behind` on its own is predicted inert for the same reason (nothing in the window
is a span), and that is a prediction, not a run.

Two things follow, one of them not about rules:

- **The managed arm is a rule-C result.** What §*The third arm* attributed to the three
  flags together belongs to `--prune-results`: it converted a 3-evictions session into a
  0-evictions, 4-prunes one, did not raise the answer count, and made the model read
  `context.rs` 26 times against 19.
- **The baseline reproduces.** This arm ran on a server restarted since the plain arm
  (same model, same flags, cold cache) and gave a bit-identical transcript at
  `--temperature 0 --seed 1`. So at temperature 0 the 8192-plain numbers are a property of
  the model and the corpus and not of one sampling path; what stays n=1 is the corpus,
  not the draw.

**Still open, added.** `--prune-behind` alone, to turn the prediction into a number; and
`--prune-results` without `--repeat-once`, to check that the managed arm's figures do not
depend on rule A firing on something the trace does not show.
